# 2507.01903 · AI4Research: A Survey of Artificial Intelligence for Scientific Research

Chen, Yang, Qin, Che et al. (HIT, CSU, HKU, UIUC, Princeton, ByteDance Seed …) · v2 5 Aug 2025 · 121 pp (48 pp body, rest references)
Read: abstract, definitions (§1-2, p5-9), taxonomy (Fig. 2, p7), theory analysis and experiment conduction (§5.3-5.5, p20-25), contents of frontiers (§10)

## Summary
The first broad survey to define **AI4Research** (AI applied to the research process
itself) as distinct from AI4Science (AI inside a domain). Its contribution is a
taxonomy of **5 task families**, a formal composition of them, a catalogue of systems
per family, and a list of frontiers. It is a map, not an evaluation: it compiles other
papers' numbers and runs nothing itself.

## The taxonomy (p5-9)
1. **Scientific Comprehension**: textual, and table/chart understanding (PaperQA2 sits here)
2. **Academic Survey**: related-work retrieval, overview/survey generation (STORM,
   AutoSurvey, SurveyForge)
3. **Scientific Discovery**: idea mining → novelty and significance assessment →
   theory analysis (claim formalisation, evidence collection, verification, theorem
   proving) → experiment conduction (design, pre-experiment estimation, management,
   conduction, analysis) → full-automatic discovery
4. **Academic Writing**: semi-automatic assistance and full-automatic writing
5. **Academic Peer Reviewing**: pre-review (desk review, reviewer matching), in-review
   (review and meta-review generation), post-review (influence, promotion)

Formally the system is a composition A = A_PR ∘ A_AW ∘ A_SD ∘ A_AS ∘ A_SC (Eq. 1),
optimised for efficiency, performance and innovation (Eq. 3). Each module has its own
objective, e.g. comprehension maximises coherence + coverage (Eq. 5); discovery
maximises novelty + validity + significance (Eq. 9).

## Points worth using
- **Claim formalisation and verification are named subfields** (§5.3.1-5.3.3): template
  methods → LLM-refined templates (Valsci); SciClaims combines extraction, retrieval and
  verification in one LLM pipeline; stepwise verification (HiSS, ProToCo); Claimify
  is catalogued under Theory Analysis (Fig. 2, p7)
- On experiments: "AI scientists currently lack the validation capabilities needed for
  rigorous experimentation and high-quality manuscript production" (citing Zhu et al.,
  p21)
- Novelty judged by LLMs alone "may overestimate creativity" and homogenise ideas; the
  field is moving to human-AI collaboration for novelty assessment (p20)
- Reproduces ScienceAgentBench's table (Table 4, p24): best o1-preview + self-debug 42.2%
  success rate
- Mentions Zochi papers accepted at ACL 2025 (p25), and closed-loop "self-driving labs"
  (p23)
- Frontiers (§10): interdisciplinary models, ethics and safety, collaborative research,
  explainability and transparency, real-time adaptive experimentation, multimodal,
  multilingual

## Limitations
- Survey dated mid-2025; predates Kosmos, the Nature AI Scientist, and every 2026 system
- Breadth over depth: systems are listed with 1-2 sentences, no common evaluation
- Says little about verification infrastructure (provenance, contracts, gating), which
  is AI4Research's focus

## Relevance
- Supplies the report's **definition of the job**: AI4Research-the-project covers all 5
  families in 1 control plane (REPORT.md §2)
- Its composition A = A_PR ∘ … ∘ A_SC is a pipeline; AI4Research's contribution is what
  sits *between* the stages (typed artifacts and gates), which the survey does not model
