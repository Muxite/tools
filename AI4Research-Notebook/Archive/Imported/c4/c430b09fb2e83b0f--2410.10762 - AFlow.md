# 2410.10762 · AFlow: Automating Agentic Workflow Generation

Zhang et al. (FoundationAgents / MetaGPT team) · ICLR 2025 · 38 pp (10 body)
Read: p1-4 (motivation, formulation, design); results from abstract and Fig. 1

## Summary
Treats **workflow design as search**. A workflow is code: LLM-invoking **nodes** (model,
prompt, temperature, output format) joined by code-based **edges** (sequence, conditions,
loops). AFlow searches this space with **Monte Carlo Tree Search**, using predefined
**operators** (Generate, Format, Review & Revise, Ensemble, Test, Programmer …) as
building blocks and real execution scores as feedback.

## How it works (p2-4)
- Distinguishes **agentic workflows** (static, predefined multi-call processes) from
  **autonomous agents** (dynamic decisions); argues workflows are easier to construct
  and optimise from human experience (p3)
- MCTS loop: soft mixed-probability selection → **LLM-driven expansion** (the optimiser
  LLM edits the workflow code) → **execution evaluation** on a validation set →
  backpropagation of experience into a tree
- Generalises prior work (prompt optimisation, hyperparameter optimisation, GPTSwarm,
  ADAS) as special cases

## Results (p1-3)
- 6 benchmarks (HumanEval, MBPP, MATH, GSM8K, HotpotQA, DROP): **+5.7%** over
  manually designed methods on average and **+19.5%** over automated approaches (text p3;
  the abstract says 5.7% over SoTA baselines)
- Smaller models with AFlow workflows beat GPT-4o on some tasks at **4.55%** of its dollar
  cost (abstract)

## Relevance
- REPORT.md §3.1-3.3: the opposite direction to AI4Research. AFlow **optimises the
  workflow against a benchmark score**; AI4Research **compiles one workflow per request
  and freezes it**. AFlow's search needs a trustworthy metric; without one it would
  optimise toward whatever the evaluator rewards
- Its node definition (model, prompt, temperature, output format) is close to
  AI4Research's logical/physical operator bindings, but without effects or trust class
