# 2605.10052 · Swarm Skills: A Portable, Self-Evolving Multi-Agent System Specification for Coordination Engineering

openJiuwen Team & Gaoling School of AI, Renmin University · arXiv v2 15 May 2026 · 14 pp · read in full

## Summary
A **specification paper**. Extends the Anthropic Skills standard (`SKILL.md`) from
single agents to teams, so that a multi-agent coordination protocol becomes a portable,
distributable asset instead of framework-internal code (AutoGen scripts, CrewAI
templates, MetaGPT SOPs). Pairs the format with a self-evolution algorithm that
distils new team skills from trajectories and patches existing ones. Positions itself
inside "Coordination Engineering", a paradigm the openJiuwen community proposes as the
step after prompt, context and harness engineering (p4).

## How it works
- **Philosophy**: "a description format, not a runtime" (p5). Message passing, queues
  and process isolation are left to the Host Agent
- **5 author components** (p5-6): `SKILL.md` frontmatter (`kind: swarm-skill`,
  `teammate mode` build/plan, `roles[]` with skills, tools, optional model), a
  natural-language body, `roles/` personas, `workflow.md` (dependency graph in prose
  or Mermaid), `bind.md` (max turns, token budgets, **quality gates before completion**)
- **Evolution Experience** (`evolutions.json`, p6): records of context (the friction),
  change directive (the patch) and E/U/F scores. Patches are appended, base files stay
  untouched, "mitigating the risk of catastrophic degradation from a single erroneous
  patch" (p9)
- **Lifecycle** (p7-9): CREATE (distil a trajectory with ≥2 sub-agent roles into a
  candidate skill, staged), USE (progressive disclosure: descriptions in the leader
  prompt, bodies read on demand), PATCH (friction patterns or explicit feedback →
  new record)
- **Scoring**: S = w_E·E + w_U·U + w_F·F; Effectiveness with a Beta(1,1) prior,
  Utilization decays when the leader ignores a patch, Freshness with a ~90-day
  half-life (p9)
- **Governance** when records reach ~10: SIMPLIFY (LLM pruning), REBUILD (rewrite base
  files, archive the old version), ROLLBACK (revert to an archived version) (p9)
- **Graceful degradation**: hosts without multi-agent support ignore `kind`/`roles[]`
  and run the workflow as a single agent (p10)

## Results
- **No quantitative evaluation.** Motivation is a crawl of 9 skill repositories
  (April 2026) showing team-style single-agent skills and high installs for adversarial
  or parallel skills, e.g. critique skills with >77.8K installs (p4)
- Validation is 1 qualitative case study: a travel-planning team in JiuwenSwarm, where
  a friction analysis proposed splitting a Budget Reviewer role and a REBUILD added a
  6th role (p10-12)

## Limitations (stated)
- No large-scale conformance testing across other hosts (AutoGen, LangGraph) (p4)
- No standard benchmark for coordination quality, so self-evolution gains are
  unquantified (p4)
- "First-run lock-in": a bad initial workflow gets patched rather than restructured (p4)

## Critical notes
- The abstract claims evolution works "eliminating the need for human-in-the-loop
  oversight", yet the case study's structural change was "submitted … for user
  approval", and footnote 2 says JiuwenSwarm exposes an optional human-review step
  (p12)
- Effectiveness is updated from "the outcome of tasks executing the patch", which is
  task-outcome curation, the signal the capsule report argues admits broken artifacts
  (cf. 2604.00392 in the capsule notes)
- `bind.md` quality gates are natural language, not checked mechanically

## Relevance
- **Capsule report**: the closest openJiuwen-native analogue to a capability library
  with lifecycle (create, patch, rebuild, rollback) and none of the conformance
  checking the capsule design proposes
- **General report**: shows where openJiuwen puts coordination knowledge (portable
  prose assets) versus where AI4Research puts it (typed, frozen contracts)
