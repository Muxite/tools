# 2605.16976 · Securing LLM Agents Needs Intent-to-Execution Integrity

Qu et al. · arXiv May 2026 · position paper · 11 pp (9 body)
Read: body p1-6

## Summary
A **position paper**: agent security has no correctness property, so defences are
stacked ad hoc. It frames an agent as a **compiler** from user intent through plans and
tool calls to system operations, and defines correctness, as in compilers (CompCert),
as **semantic preservation of the user's intent**. It derives 4 properties that must hold
together, called **intent-to-execution integrity**, and scores existing defences
against them: none covers all 4.

## The argument (p2-5)
- Motivation from open skill ecosystems (OpenClaw / ClawHub): the ClawHavoc campaign
  planted >1,000 malicious skills; an audit of 42,447 skills found **26.1%** with at least
  one vulnerability; sandbox bypasses shown by Snyk (p2)
- 2 problem sources, 4 properties (Table 1, p3):
  - untrusted **data** → **Instruction Integrity** (every action attributable to the
    authenticated user), **Data Flow Integrity** (taint-marked sensitive data may not flow
    to unauthorised sinks, across sessions), **Judgment Integrity** (decisions invariant to
    task-irrelevant perturbation: J(q, d ⊕ δ) = J(q, d))
  - untrusted **tools** → **Tool Integrity** (every component confined to its **declared
    interface and documented behaviour**; no hidden side effects, unadvertised endpoints,
    or writes outside scope) (p4)
- Sufficiency by case analysis: every agent step is reasoning, action construction, or
  execution, and each corruption path is closed by one property (p4-5)
- Defence coverage (Table 3, p5): CaMeL and Progent provide provable coverage of
  instruction and data-flow integrity but not judgment or tool integrity; OpenClaw-specific
  defences are partial everywhere
