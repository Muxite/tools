# Scientific workflow transfer: protocol evidence and execution evidence

Scope: three primary papers not already covered in the shared DiscoveryBench/PaperBench/AI Scientist cards. This is optional M2 research, not a design requirement. I inspected the linked full text, including methods/results/limitations; source facts and our inferences are separated below.

## AutoLabs: benchmarked procedure generation, narrow hardware execution

**Gihan Panapitiya et al., “AutoLabs: cognitive multi-agent systems with self-correction for autonomous chemical experimentation,” Scientific Reports 16, 19554 (published 25 June 2026).** [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-45593-z). Full text: “Agent architecture,” “Evaluation experiments,” “RAG pipeline,” “Qualitative expert case study,” and “Hardware validation.”

**Reported evidence.** AutoLabs converts natural-language chemical experiment requests into Big Kahuna liquid-handler instructions using a supervisor and specialist agents, calculation tools, iterative checks, and rule-based hardware-file generation. The paper says instrument constraints are conveyed in natural-language prompts, not formal schemas or programmatic validation; generated procedures receive critical human verification before execution. Across five manually specified benchmark protocols and 20 configurations (10 runs each), the authors compare step-level F1 and chemical-quantity error. In a retrieval ablation using three validated prior procedures, RAG improves `MA (PR)` on average (overall F1 +0.044) but worsens `MA-TU-GSC (PR)` (overall F1 −0.024; chemical F1 −0.034; quantity nRMSE worsens by 0.028). These are not a clean architecture-only contrast: the latter also enables tool use and guided self-check. Expert review finds omitted mixing/cooling steps and an inserted delay that disables vial timers; some failures reflect prompt omissions. Hardware instructions loaded successfully for experiments 1–4, with occasional multi-plate issues for experiment 5; five experiments ran in simulation, while only experiments 1–2 were physically run end-to-end. These results assess protocol agreement/importability; they do not establish our gate, authority enforcement, or general outcome validity. The [author repository](https://github.com/pnnl/autolabs) is a lead only; we did not run its code.

**Our inference.** Reuse should be evaluated against condition compatibility and outcome checks, not mere topical similarity. Procedural steps omitted by the request expose an important distinction between “matches stated instructions” and “satisfies domain/workflow postconditions.” This supports a test of stale/incompatible procedure feeds; it does not prove a particular retrieval architecture.

## Coscientist: distinct evidence classes, including an instrument-confirmed outcome

**Daniil A. Boiko, Robert MacKnight, Ben Kline and Gabe Gomes, “Autonomous chemical research with large language models,” Nature 624, 570–578 (published 20 December 2023).** [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06792-0). Full text: “Controlling laboratory hardware,” “Integrated chemical experiment design,” “Chemical reasoning capabilities”; linked Supplementary Information sections “Results of the HPLC experiment in the cloud lab” and “Results of the experimental study.” [Author repository](https://github.com/gomesgroup/coscientist) is a lead; it was not run, and the article notes the public implementation is simpler than the system described.

**Reported evidence.** The paper reports several different levels of experimental evidence. In a toy plate-reader task, Coscientist identified three color samples from spectra after a guiding prompt. At cloud lab ECL, its generated HPLC code executed on caffeine standards, but ECL software selected the column/mobile phase/gradient and an air bubble entered with the analyte, exposing a quality-control failure. In the integrated Suzuki and Sonogashira task, the system wrote OT-2 code, initially used a wrong heater-shaker method name, retrieved documentation, corrected it, and ran successfully; plates were moved manually. Subsequent GC-MS spectra matched the target products/reference spectra for both reactions. By contrast, the paper’s reaction “optimization” experiments queried fully mapped datasets: proposed reactions were already represented as lookup-table entries, so these were not a new wet-lab campaign. These result classes should not be conflated as evidence of autonomous discovery or generalized protocol reliability.

**Our inference.** This is a useful evidence ladder for an experiment trace: successful tool call, physical execution, instrument output, and scientifically interpreted outcome are separate claims. The GC-MS result supports the specific products reported, not general transfer. The lookup experiment tests reasoning over known tabulated outcomes, not live experimental iteration.

## RoboCulture: long physical execution with explicit human boundary

**Kevin Angers et al., “RoboCulture: A Robotics Platform for Automated Biological Experimentation,” arXiv:2505.14941v2 [cs.RO], version dated 24 May 2025.** [Versioned preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14941v2). A later article is [“RoboCulture enables modular robotic plate-based cell automation,” Cell Reports Physical Science 7(7), 103335 (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrp.2026.103335). Full preprint text: §§II-B–II-F, III, IV-F; code and generated data are linked in §IV-A3.

**Reported evidence.** RoboCulture integrates robot vision, force-guided pipette tip exchange, liquid handling, optical-density tracking, and reactive behavior trees. The authors report component tests, including 576 randomized target-well insertions (99% success when retrying up to three times is allowed; 92.4% without retry) and a 36-cycle tip attach/remove test (100%). An integrated 15-hour yeast-culture run monitored wells every five minutes; six replicate wells per initial-density group showed daughter-well growth patterns similar to parent groups, while blank wells showed no growth. The authors compared camera-derived trends against plate-reader measurements from an identical plate prepared simultaneously by a skilled human operator, reporting reasonable agreement. The lowest-density group had not saturated by 12 hours, so an operator instructed an early split; initial cell seeding was also manual. Note a paper-internal qualification: Results §II-F says the 50M and 30M groups were split at detected saturation, but Discussion §III says splitting was manually initiated as a safety precaution. Treat the degree of autonomous split triggering as ambiguous. Authors note image-derived optical density assumes stable lighting and the workflow remains task-specific.

**Our inference.** This is a useful model for evaluating an experiment as a trace with retries, observations, interventions, and external outcome checks—not as a single success flag. It is one physical demonstration, not evidence of broad protocol transfer or autonomous scientific discovery. The human-triggered early split is a valid resource-boundary case to preserve in evaluation rather than silently scoring as autonomous completion.

## Candidate M2 experiment (proposal, not a gate)

Create a small blinded suite of protocols with (a) a valid current procedure, (b) a relevant but condition-mismatched prior procedure, (c) a known stale procedure, and (d) no reusable procedure. Freeze each source procedure and annotate its conditions, source, and known postconditions. Run repeated tasks in simulation first, then only where permitted on low-risk hardware. Score protocol completeness and parameter correctness separately from import/simulation success and measured outcome. Include deliberate interruption/retry and one resource-limit intervention; retain failures and negative outcomes. An independent checker should compare instrument logs and scientific measurements to preregistered acceptance criteria. Report all runs, including manual assists, per-condition transfer, false success, time, and cost. This tests whether provenance/condition checks help and where they fail; it does not select a production policy.

## Evidence gaps

None of these papers establishes safe generalization of remembered procedures across changed reagents, instruments, labs, or biological conditions. AutoLabs physically validates only two of its five benchmark experiments and reports imported/simulated execution for the rest. RoboCulture’s external check is a single, same-time comparison with a human-prepared plate; normalized growth trends are not independent proof of all biological outcomes. These sources cannot establish general reliability, reproducibility across laboratories, or that an agent should autonomously select or execute a protocol.
