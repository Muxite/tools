# 2604.03173 · Detecting and Correcting Reference Hallucinations in Commercial LLMs and Deep Research Agents (urlhealth)

Rao, Wong, Callison-Burch · University of Pennsylvania · arXiv 3 Apr 2026 · 25 pp (9 body)
Read: body p1-6 and results tables

## Summary
Measures whether citation **URLs** from LLMs and deep-research agents exist at all.
221,111 URLs: 53,090 from 10 systems on DRBench (DeepResearch Bench) and 168,021 from 3
models on ExpertQA across 32 fields. A URL is **non-resolving** if it errors (403
excluded as bot-blocking), and **hallucinated** if it is non-resolving and has **no
Wayback Machine snapshot ever**; stale = existed once, now dead (p2-3).

## Results
- **3-13% of citation URLs hallucinated; 5-18% non-resolving** (p1). Per model on DRBench
  (Table 2, p4): Claude-3.5-sonnet 3.0% hallucinated; OpenAI Deep Research 3.5%
  hallucinated but 10.1% non-resolving; **Gemini-2.5-Pro Deep Research 13.3% hallucinated,
  18.5% non-resolving** despite a top DRBench quality score
- **Deep-research agents hallucinate more than search-augmented LLMs**: pooled 10.7% vs
  4.8% (p < 10⁻⁵¹), while producing far more citations (113.1 and 41.2 URLs per query vs
  3.0-24.3) (p5)
- More citations do not mean fewer errors per citation (RQ5)
- Field effects: 5.4% (Business) to 11.4% (Theology) non-resolving; Healthcare/Medicine
  worst for Claude Sonnet 4.5 at 17.4% (p5)
- **Mitigation**: giving models the `urlhealth` tool for self-correction cuts
  non-resolving URLs 6-79× to under 1%, dependent on tool-use competence (p1-2)

## Limitations (stated)
- Hallucination rate is a **lower bound** (incomplete Wayback coverage; 403s excluded)
- Only URL existence: fabricated snippets and invented bibliographic metadata are
  "documented qualitatively but require separate systematic study" (p2)

## Relevance
- REPORT.md §3.5: a clean, large-sample failure rate for citations, and a demonstration
  that a **deterministic check** (does the URL exist, was it ever archived) fixes a
  failure that prompting does not
- The class it leaves open, **fabricated snippets** (quote not on the cited page), is the
  one AI4Research's literature path addresses for papers it has ingested: a claim gets a
  literature verdict only if its text occurs in the cited section, and only spans whose
  `source_text_sha256` re-verifies are passed to the report
- Fairness: its 3-13% applies to free-generating LLMs and deep-research agents, not to
  retrieval-grounded systems like PaperQA2 or OpenScholar
