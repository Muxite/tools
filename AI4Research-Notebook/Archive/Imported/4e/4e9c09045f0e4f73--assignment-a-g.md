# The assignment: a to g, as Muk defines them (2026-09-22)

The design must answer seven requirements. Here is what each letter means, in Muk's words where given. Binding
directives: `tundle/obby/Directives.md`.

| Letter | Requirement | What the answer must contain |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | Function | CC makes capabilities easier to **pick, verify, improve and remove**. Say what makes each easier, from the schema |
| **b** | Architecture | The schema's fields and what each should be, including how the capability itself (skill, tool, MCP, A2A) is held; the records; the two trust levels; required versus optional parts |
| **c** | Position | Where CC sits in the system: the workflow's **planning, freeze and dispatch** stages; what it touches in the code; with the research behind it |
| **d** | Dependencies | What **CC itself** depends on: agent-core code, Symphony, the workflow's stages, materials, other teams and models. Not a capability's internal dependencies: the schema handles those through pinning, or through unpinning with a written purpose |
| **e** | Similar designs | **Symphony first**, then other similar designs, then papers with similar traits. Most of them *observe*; CC *declares* |
| **f** | Advantages | Why the combination is better than those, with what would prove each advantage wrong |
| **g** | Feasibility | Much of this has been built before, but never combined into one schema: what exists, what is new, the order to build it |

**Structure required.** Key slides at the start answer each letter. The body then elaborates each part and why. The
rationale is organised by function: "we do X because of paper A".
