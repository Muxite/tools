# M2 research: workflows, tools, and scientific execution

**Research snapshot: 7 October 2026.** This is an evidence note for the next milestone (M2), not architecture or acceptance authority. Paper dates below distinguish the first public version from conference/journal publication where verified. Claims are based on the linked primary papers, not the benchmark names alone.

## Human synopsis

The strongest repeated signal is that long workflows need explicit state, verifiable intermediate outcomes, and retained traces: final-answer correctness alone makes it hard to locate a failed handoff or distinguish a lucky success from repeatable behavior. Benchmarks report large gaps between isolated tool-call competence and end-to-end success; multi-agent studies find failures in specification, handoffs, and termination, while adding roles/prompts gives mixed and modest improvements. Scientific automation work shows credible narrow experiments and rapid code iteration, but does not establish general autonomous science; tasks, protocols, measurements, baselines, budgets, and failure records still bound what a result means.

A practical M1-compatible carry-forward is to make every experiment replayable from a frozen protocol, exact inputs/environment, raw observations, and explicit run outcome; retain full attempt/decision/tool traces and inspect failure causes. The current design already calls for those properties in its contracts, durable evidence, frozen hypothesis protocol, fail-closed gate, and stage exits. M2 can test whether additional workflow mechanisms—checkpoint/restart, smaller subgoals, or parallel agents—improve measured completion/reproducibility without introducing untracked retries or weakening the existing boundary.

## What evidence supports

- **Empirical findings:** named results below are reported on each paper’s specific task set, model versions, harness, and scoring method. They do not establish universal rates for AI4Research.
- **Design inference:** the suggested M2 comparisons are implications to test, not findings proved by these studies. A benchmark result about simulated support/retail/code is not direct evidence for scientific workflows.
- **Existing design coverage:** Workflow already freezes graph, versions, protocol and inputs; records attributed attempts; durably gates successor release; defaults to zero repair/replay; distinguishes scientific rejection from infrastructure failure. Sidecar already calls for bounded execution, exact target/tool/environment identity, timing, truthful outcomes, evidence retention, and ambiguous-submit handling. Research suggestions should extend/validate these, not silently replace them.

## Evidence-backed literature

### Long scientific workflows and experiment quality

1. **[DiscoveryBench: Towards Data-Driven Discovery with Large Language Models](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/0d70af566e69f1dfb687791ecf955e28-Paper-Conference.pdf)** — Majumder et al.; arXiv first posted 1 July 2024; ICLR 2025. Primary full text inspected (benchmark design/results, pp. 1–5). 264 manually derived tasks across six domains plus 903 synthetic tasks; each task includes dataset, metadata and natural-language discovery goal; facet-based evaluation decomposes failures. Best tested system scores 25%. Useful because it tests the chain from interpreting goal and dataset through discovery, not just code execution. **Limit:** data-driven discovery benchmark; limited domain/task coverage and LLM baselines do not prove experimental validity in physical science. **Design implication:** freeze an empirical baseline, data provenance, preprocessing and outcome/negative criteria before candidate work; score each obligation at its consumer boundary. That inference complements the current frozen Hypothesis Blueprint/protocol.

2. **[RE-Bench: Evaluating frontier AI R&D capabilities of language model agents against human experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15114)** — Wijk et al.; first posted 22 Nov 2024. Primary text inspected (design, human comparison, results, discussion). Seven open-ended ML research-engineering environments; 71 eight-hour attempts by 61 human experts. AI agents exceed average human results under a 2-hour total budget in their best-of-k setup, but experts' returns to time are better and the top human result is about 2x the best agent after 32 hours. The paper emphasizes task/environment feasibility, coverage, non-saturation and equal-condition comparison. **Limit:** only seven relatively small ML tasks; its authors say real AI R&D is at least two orders larger across dimensions; result is scaffold/model/time-budget specific. **Design implication:** compare with a fixed, executable reference and human-checked oracle; report per-task, repeat and budget curves, not a single average or cherry-picked best run.

3. **[PaperBench: Evaluating AI’s Ability to Replicate AI Research](https://cdn.openai.com/papers/22265bac-3191-44e5-b057-7aaacd8e90cd/paperbench.pdf)** — Starace et al.; 2 Apr 2025 release/preprint. Primary full text inspected (abstract, introduction, rubric/evaluation setup). Evaluates 20 ICML 2024 Spotlight/Oral paper replications from scratch with 8,316 hierarchically decomposed, author-co-developed gradable outcomes and a separate judge benchmark. Best tested agent scores 21% average; human PhDs outperform models on the evaluated subset. **Limit:** AI/ML-only papers and rubric/LLM-judge evaluation; replication success is not novelty or scientific truth. **Design implication:** make evaluation obligations hierarchical and partial progress inspectable; validate the evaluator separately against expert labels and preserve exact code/results as evidence.

4. **[The AI Scientist: Towards Fully Automated Open-Ended Scientific Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06292)** — Lu et al.; first posted 12 Aug 2024. Primary full text inspected (workflow, evaluation, limitations, and ethical considerations). An end-to-end system proposes ideas, codes/runs experiments, visualizes results, drafts papers and simulates review on three ML topics. The authors directly report similar ideas across runs/models; a significant fraction of proposed ideas not implemented; incorrect implementations that can be hard to catch; and too few experiments to establish expected rigor or fair parameter/FLOP/runtime comparisons, sometimes producing deceptive or inaccurate conclusions. It also describes an agent saving every update checkpoint until storage approached 1 TB and attempting to alter imposed time limits; authors recommend strict sandboxing. **Limit:** original evaluation uses small ML templates; its reviewer validation uses ICLR 2022 data and cannot evaluate figures or ask authors questions. **Design implication:** independently verify implementations, measurements, fair controls and resource limits; preserve raw runs and distinguish the artifact from its self-review.

5. **[Towards end-to-end automation of AI research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10265-5)** — Lu et al.; published in Nature on 25 Mar 2026. Primary article and supplement discussed, including submission protocol and limitations. Of three manually filtered AI-generated manuscripts submitted to the ICLR 2025 ICBINB workshop, one scored above the workshop's average acceptance threshold (6.33 from three reviewers; top 45%) and organizers said it likely would have been accepted; it was withdrawn per a pre-established protocol. The paper reports that none met the main-conference bar, and names naive/underdeveloped ideas, incorrect implementations, weak methodology, experimental errors and inaccurate citations as failure modes. **Limit:** one bounded computational-ML workshop case, with manual selection and deliberate withdrawal; it demonstrates neither general scientific quality nor consistent performance. **Design implication:** separate evidence for execution, result correctness, novelty, and paper quality; no single verifier score should stand in for all four.


### Reliability, coordination, and observable tools

6. **[Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?](https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/b1041e52d3be19f0a9bc491657488e4a-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html)** — Cemri et al.; first posted 17 Mar 2025, NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks. Primary full text inspected (methods, prevalence, intervention case studies). Human-annotated 150+ traces establish 14 failure modes (specification/system design, inter-agent misalignment, verification/termination; κ=.88); authors scale analysis to 1,642 traces from seven frameworks with LLM labeling (94% accuracy, κ=.77 on held-out set). Role/prompt and topology changes help some outcomes, but effects vary and fixes are modest/incomplete; verification remains a persistent weakness. **Limit:** benchmark traces/tasks and LLM-as-judge labels; taxonomy is not exhaustive, and the interventions are small case studies. **Design implication:** inspect trajectories and handoffs by failure mode, not only aggregate pass rate; test role prompts/topology as controlled treatments, not presumed remedies. This directly supports the current gate/runner evidence design while identifying observability/diagnosis as a likely M2 measurement opportunity.

7. **[AgentBoard: An Analytical Evaluation Board of Multi-turn LLM Agents](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/877b40688e330a0e2a3fc24084208dfa-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)** — Ma et al.; arXiv first posted 24 Jan 2024; NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks. Primary paper text inspected (benchmark definition and task design). Argues final success rate hides where agents stall; pairs multi-round, partially observable tasks with fine-grained progress rates and trajectory/skill visualizations. **Limit:** simulated general-agent tasks; progress metrics can diagnose partial completion but cannot establish scientific correctness or evidence adequacy. **Design implication:** M2 trace views should show accepted/blocked boundary, tool calls, attempts, budget, and progress toward explicit obligations; metrics should stay subordinate to final gate criteria.

8. **[MINT: Evaluating LLMs in Multi-turn Interaction with Tools and Language Feedback](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/8a0d3ae989a382ce6e50312bc35bf7e1-Paper-Conference.pdf)** — Wang et al.; arXiv first posted 19 Sep 2023; ICLR 2024. Primary text inspected (sampling, setup, tables). On a 586-instance, stratified set across reasoning, code, and ALFWorld, each extra tool turn raised success by 1–8 points and simulated language feedback by 2–17 points; single-turn rank did not predict multi-turn results, and some evaluated fine-tuned models did worse. They use a controlled interaction cap and explicitly note evaluation-cost/reproducibility tradeoffs when feedback is simulated. **Limit:** curated subset from existing benchmarks and GPT-simulated feedback, older model versions, five-turn cap. **Design implication:** test the whole workflow across repeated runs and realistic failures; do not infer end-to-end reliability from one-shot model scores or a single successful trace.

9. **[τ-bench: A Benchmark for Tool-Agent-User Interaction in Real-World Domains](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/1b126cc38b8638e07bef37e7b2bb72bf-Paper-Conference.pdf)** — Yao, Shinn, Razavi & Narasimhan; arXiv first posted 17 Jun 2024; ICLR 2025. Primary full text inspected (state model, metric, result). Uses stateful API databases, domain policies and a simulated user; exact database-state plus response checks score outcomes. The authors define pass@k as at least one successful trial among k and pass^k (“pass hat k”) as all k successful; trials are modeled as i.i.d. For GPT-4o, mean pass^1 is 48.2% across retail and airline, while retail pass^8 is below 25%. **Limit:** retail/airline customer service; simulated users and a task-specific state oracle. A necessary final state can still miss policy violations, which authors acknowledge; strict reproducibility/science task generalization is unproven. **Design implication:** report raw outcomes and consistency across repeats alongside any pass@k-like summary; include state/effect oracles and policy-adherence checks independent from narrative quality.

10. **[ToolSandbox: A Stateful, Conversational, Interactive Evaluation Benchmark for LLM Tool Use Capabilities](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.65/)** — Lu et al.; first posted 8 Aug 2024; Findings of NAACL 2025. Primary text inspected (design and scenario categories). Extends evaluation beyond stateless calls with stateful tools, implicit dependencies, an on-policy user simulator, milestone checks on arbitrary trajectories, and scenarios for insufficient information, canonicalization, and state dependency. **Limit:** synthetic benchmark domains do not establish operational safety. **Design implication:** sidecar/product validation can include stateful scenarios: prerequisite sequencing, malformed or missing inputs, partial progress, failure/cancellation, and final-state reconciliation. Existing sidecar already requires bounded jobs, truthful partial-evidence outcomes and target/effect controls.

11. **[On Training Large Language Models for Long-Horizon Tasks: An Empirical Study of Horizon Length](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.02572)** — Kim et al.; arXiv first posted 4 May 2026; ICML 2026. Primary full text inspected (controlled task construction, results, robustness checks, limitations). Separates shortest goal distance from action budget and policy’s realized steps; controlled Sudoku/Rush Hour tasks show RL instability as required action horizon grows. Macro-actions and subgoal decomposition stabilize training; WebShop, a 4B model, and GRPO-style ablations support some robustness. **Limit:** trained mainly one 1.7B model family and puzzle-like environments; this is a training-dynamics result, not direct proof that M2’s orchestration should compress workflows. **M2 hypothesis:** compare atomic versus grouped/checkpointed scientific substeps on a fixed task while preserving all evidence/gates; see whether fewer handoffs reduce errors without hiding intermediate failures or increasing invalid final results.

12. **[ReAct: Synergizing Reasoning and Acting in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)** — Yao et al.; arXiv first posted 6 Oct 2022; ICLR 2023. Primary full text inspected (method, benchmarks, limitations). Interleaves reasoning, actions and external observations; on ALFWorld/WebShop it reports +34/+10 absolute success-rate gains over imitation/RL baselines with 1–2 examples, and observed benefits over reasoning-only/acting-only ablations. **Limit:** few-shot prompting on bounded QA/game/shopping environments with PaLM-era models; authors note long/action-rich tasks need more demonstrations and context can limit them. A readable rationale trace is not a system-of-record or correctness proof. **Design implication:** expose tool observations and explicit plan updates for diagnosis, but separately preserve authoritative tool outputs, accepted artifact provenance and protected gate decisions.

## M2 hypotheses worth testing (not adopted requirements)

1. **Checkpointed bounded workflow:** on matched, frozen research tasks, compare the current sequential path with explicit durable checkpoints and resumable execution after injected process/transport loss. Measure end-to-end and per-boundary completion, time/cost, duplicate side effects, evidence completeness, and recovery correctness. Keep interruption distinct from failure; a checkpoint must never substitute for gate acceptance. Existing architecture already requires restart to preserve accepted work and pause interrupted attempts, so the novel question is recovery behavior/benefit at realistic long-run boundaries.
2. **Repeatability and negative science:** rerun fixed protocols with a locked environment and varied seeds; report outcome consistency, infrastructure interruptions, and whether prespecified positive, negative, and inconclusive cases route correctly. This tests scientific quality rather than assuming every valid run discovers a positive result. Current docs already require positive/negative demonstrations and frozen protocol; M2 can add meaningful repeated-run variance accounting.
3. **Trace-assisted diagnosis:** deliberately inject a small set of handoff/tool/persistence failures; have an operator classify root cause using ordinary logs versus a process trace showing node/attempt, tool arguments (with secrets redacted), environment pins, observation, state/effect, budget, gate verdict and commit. Compare diagnosis time/accuracy and evidence reconstruction. This is a tooling-quality experiment, not product-agent authority. Current sidecar already aims to retain reproducible failure details; test whether its minimum evidence is sufficient.
4. **Coordination ablation, only if M2 needs multiple agents:** use identical objective/data/budget and compare solo, fixed sequential specialist roles, and bounded independent proposal+referee arrangements. Track handoff loss, duplicated work, agreement/error correlation, false pass, cost, and reproducibility. A “more agents” score alone is not success. MAST shows coordination failures and verifier limitations; existing M1 explicitly defers Agent Team/Cluster integration until the fixed research graph is operational and plan verification/freeze exist.

## Useful anti-patterns to prohibit in experiments

- **Planner self-approval / proposal execution before freeze:** confuses candidate planning with protected binding and authorization. Existing M1 already says no.
- **Agent declares “done” with no consumer-level acceptance:** MAST finds task verification/termination defects; require output-to-obligation evaluation and durable gate commit. Existing gate architecture covers this.
- **Role prompt or debate treated as independent verification:** same-model critique can share correlated errors; MAST’s verifier category stays high across systems, and role/topology interventions vary. Independent invocation helps process separation but does not prove statistical independence.
- **Automatic retry/repair after uncertain side effects:** loses failure attribution and can duplicate writes. Current workflow/sidecar already says ambiguous execution is not blindly retried; preserve as explicit test case.
- **Best-of-N reported as run reliability:** pass@k means at least one of k trials succeeds; τ-bench writes pass^k (read “pass hat k”) for all k trials succeeding. Its formula assumes i.i.d. trials. Report raw per-run outcomes and the chosen summary; for science experiments, do not assume repeated runs are independent merely because a benchmark does. Correlated seeds, shared state or shared model errors can make all-success consistency misleading.
- **Final-artifact-only evaluation:** hides process failures and method quality; preserve raw observation/tool/evidence trace, but keep model-generated traces distinct from verified environment facts.
- **Success-only scientific outcomes:** excludes valid null/falsifying or inconclusive results and biases the apparent workflow score. The protocol, not the agent’s post-hoc framing, determines scientific interpretation.
- **Aggregate score detached from budget, task scope, model/scaffold and environment:** PaperBench/RE-Bench illustrate that time budget, scaffold, ceiling and task details change conclusions. Keep candidate/environment pins and matched conditions with every comparison.
