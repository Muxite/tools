# 2605.08956 · Agentic AI Scientists Are Not Built For Autonomous Scientific Discovery

Bisht, Kumar, Jablonka, Mausam, Krishnan · IIT Delhi / FSU Jena · arXiv May 2026 ·
20 pp (9 body, references at p10) · position paper
Read: p1-9 in full. Appendix A (hivemind experiment details) not read.

## Summary
Argues that current agentic AI scientists work as **co-scientists** but are structurally
unfit for **autonomous** discovery, for four upstream reasons that scale and scaffolding
do not fix: (1) problem selection follows the McNamara fallacy toward what is
measurable; (2) training corpora omit the tacit procedural and failure knowledge of lab
practice; (3) preference optimisation compresses hypothesis diversity toward consensus;
(4) benchmarks are single-turn and have no feedback from physical experiment. Runs one
experiment ("hypothesis hivemind") and closes with six design recommendations, one of
which is the mechanism the report says no system has.

## How it works (the argument, p2-7)
- Running example throughout is solid-state electrolyte (SSE) discovery for Li-ion
  conductivity; the prediction is that any current system will propose LLZO/NASICON
  variants because that is what the literature over-represents (p5)
- §3 McNamara: cites a study where AI-augmented scientists publish 3.02× more papers and
  get 4.84× more citations while the collective volume of topics studied shrinks 4.63%
  and scientist-to-scientist engagement falls 22% (p3)
- §5 Diversity: RLHF/DPO shrink the output distribution toward annotator consensus;
  distillation propagates a teacher's bias through number-sequence data alone; model
  collapse compounds across generations (p5)
- ⭐ **Hypothesis hivemind experiment** (p5-6, Fig. 1): 50 NeurIPS 2025 AI4Mat papers;
  six models from two providers (Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 4.5, Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5 nano/mini/5).
  Task A: recover the hypothesis from an experiment summary (convergence expected);
  Task B: propose new hypotheses from the full paper (diversity expected). Average
  cross-model cosine similarity in Task A is 0.72-0.91; in Task B it is still
  **0.57-0.79**. "The effective epistemic sample size ... is close to one regardless of
  how many systems are consulted"
- §6 Benchmarks: cites CORE-Bench Hard vs ReplicationBench disagreement, a chest X-ray
  VQA model scoring top without the image, and Ríos-García et al. 2026's >25,000 agent
  runs across eight domains where **evidence was ignored in 68% of traces, refutation-
  driven belief revision occurred in 26%**, and the base model rather than the scaffold
  explained 41.4% of behavioural variance (p6). Also: of GNoME's 2.2 million predicted
  structures, 736 had been experimentally realised at publication (p7)

## Recommendations (p8-9)
- Public reviews of papers and proposals as training data for scientific judgment
- ⭐ **Public preregistration of AI-generated hypotheses**: "AI scientists be required to
  register hypotheses in a structured, machine-readable repository before any
  simulation or automated laboratory test is initiated. Unlike human researchers, this
  is easy to implement via an API call in the experimental pipeline making
  circumvention a deliberate act against scientific integrity" (p9). Argued on the
  ground that automated hypothesis-testing loops "can amplify [p-hacking] by orders of
  magnitude" (p8)
- Training corpora that encode failure and tacit knowledge (lab video, negative-result
  venues)
- Distribution-matching post-training to preserve diversity
- Simulators (OpenMM, GROMACS, MuJoCo) as scalable verifiers and training oracles
- Persistent, mutable world models of the agent's epistemic state (p9)

## Limitations
- Position paper: the only original evidence is the hivemind experiment, which is
  embedding-similarity only, has no human-generated baseline for what "diverse enough"
  looks like, and tests two providers' models on one materials-science track
- Most quantitative claims are secondary citations (Ríos-García 2026, Kapoor &
  Narayanan 2023, Cheetham & Seshadri 2024); none are reproduced here
- The "alternative views" section (p7-8) is argued, not tested

## Relevance
- ⭐ **Directly relevant to the report's integrity-hash claim**: this is the paper that
  *asks for* pre-run sealing of hypotheses at repository scale, argues it is trivially
  implementable inside an agent pipeline, and notes no system does it. It confirms the
  gap the report identifies and supplies the strongest independent rationale for
  closing it (automated p-hacking at scale)
- The 68% / 26% figures (p6) are the best available quantification of "weak at checking"
  at the trace level; cite via this paper but note they originate in Ríos-García 2026
- The hivemind result undercuts any verification design that relies on *ensembles of
  frontier models* as independent reviewers — the report's Tier VIII discussion should
  note that cross-model agreement is not independent evidence
- Its four "upstream" failures map onto the AI4Research survey's lifecycle stages and
  give a principled reason why benchmark scores in §4 do not transfer to autonomy
