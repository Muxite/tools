# Verification reliability and bounded RSI: literature audit (2026-10-07)

**Status:** bounded literature audit complete for this pass. This is a local ignored research note, not design authority. Sources below are primary papers or their author-maintained repositories; versions and dates identify the latest arXiv versions checked on 2026-10-07. Claims are limited to their reported tasks and setups. Literature does not establish that the specific JiuwenSwarm architecture is correct.

## Human synopsis

The papers support a cautious division of labor already present in the design: deterministic checks can establish identity and format, and compare trusted runtime observations with policy; enforcing permissions depends on the trusted runtime and complete observations. A semantic model supplies a bounded assessment; protected host logic alone interprets the assessment into release. An LLM assessor is neither an oracle nor made reliable by being separate from the producer. Evidence-backed risks include order/verbosity/self-preference biases, context-induced reasoning mistakes, direct prompt-injection attacks against judges, and overoptimization against imperfect scores. There is a useful literature tension: LLM judges can align well with human preference in some open-ended comparisons, yet adversarial instruction-following and objectively correct knowledge/reasoning judgments remain difficult in dedicated meta-evaluations. Some self-feedback loops improve task performance, but gains depend on useful feedback and a valid task metric; intrinsic self-correction can also fail or regress. Held-out evaluation reduces direct feedback leakage but does not guarantee independence from exposure, repeated adaptive queries, or evaluator error.

| [Hossain, Yousefi & Lim, 2026-09-18 (v1 preprint), *Agreement Overstates Evidence: Error Dependence in LLM Judge Consensus*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22512) | §§2–4 and Appendix M: primary ten-logical-judge bank (five 7–9B models x two prompt styles) has mean pairwise error correlation 0.206 and design-effect n_eff=3.51 on the 1,133 RewardBench v2 pairs answered by all judges. On 1,885 UltraFeedback pairs, n_eff=3.36; on 635 PKU-SafeRLHF pairs, n_eff=2.89. Separate three-judge frontier bank has error correlation 0.56 and 91–93% accuracy on common RewardBench/PKU subsets. Pooled-vote vs item-level tests diverged in 28% of resampled 100-pair Focus panels. n_eff is a variance/design-effect approximation, not interchangeable judges or a majority-accuracy guarantee. | Very recent, not yet peer-reviewed (as of this audit). Preference judgments, finite and changing judge banks; position bias is the main shared vulnerability experimentally studied. Authors caution controlled interventions do not establish natural prevalence and estimates may not transfer. | Do not count same-call/provider/model-family agreement as multiple independent confirmations. If panels are used in M2, estimate error covariance on trusted profile-specific cases and predefine aggregation. Never let quorum replace required evidence or host gate logic. |
| [Madaan et al., 2023-03-30 (v2 revised 2023-05-25), *Self-Refine: Iterative Refinement with Self-Feedback*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651) | §§2–3 and Appendix H: same model generates, critiques, and revises, capped at four iterations; authors report improvements across seven tasks. | [Huang et al., 2023-10-03 (v2 revised 2024-03-14), *Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798) | Main paper’s intrinsic-self-correction experiments/conclusion: without external feedback, prompting a model to reconsider its reasoning often does not improve and can degrade performance. | Addresses intrinsic correction on reasoning tasks; it does not establish that every self-feedback task must regress. Self-Refine uses different tasks/prompts/metrics, so this is not a matched contradiction; external-evidence refinement is another distinct setting. | Distinguish “reflect again” from correction driven by new trustworthy evidence. | [Gao, Schulman & Hilton, 2022-10-19 (v1), *Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760) | §§1–3.6: in a synthetic setup, optimizing a learned proxy reward (via RL or best-of-n) eventually reduces a fixed gold reward; the proxy/gold gap grows under optimization. KL penalty behaved like earlier stopping in their setup; authors caution hyperparameter sensitivity. | Gold score is a fixed synthetic reward model rather than human/scientific judgment; empirical scaling relations are setup-specific. | A rising score on the optimization/scoring adapter alone cannot establish improvement. Keep paired protected evaluation independent of proposer feedback; bound query budgets and compare quality on sealed cases, while recording proxy vs protected score trajectories. |
| [Skalse et al., 2022-09-27 (v2 revised 2025-03-05), *Defining and Characterizing Reward Hacking*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13085) | Formal result: for all stochastic policies, nontrivial proxy/true-reward pairs are generally not “unhackable”; narrower proxies do not automatically become safe. | Mathematical characterization over reward functions/policy classes, not an empirical study of this workflow. | Supports treating score hacking as a structural possibility and writing explicit threat cases (test tampering, hidden-data inference, rubric targeting), rather than assuming a narrower score is inherently robust. |
| [Singh et al., 2024-11-06 (v1), *Evaluation data contamination in LLMs: how do we measure it and (when) does it matter?*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03923) | Main empirical analysis: ConTAM across 13 benchmarks and 7 models from two model families tests whether contamination metrics correlate with downstream performance effects; finds effects vary by model/benchmark and that simplistic/large-n matching can create false negatives. | Pretraining contamination analysis, not an adaptive sealed-oracle protocol; detection metrics are imperfect and benchmark-specific. | Split labels and access controls are necessary but should be paired with exact audience/query logs, no hidden answer exposure, and analysis of score-vs-query behavior. Do not claim “unseen” solely from a filename/split name. |
| [Dwork et al., 2015-06-08 (v2 revised 2015-09-25), *Generalization in Adaptive Data Analysis and Holdout Reuse*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02629) | §§1, 4–5: repeated, adaptive querying of an ordinary holdout can overfit it; reusable-holdout guarantees rely on a specified information-control mechanism and assumptions (e.g., iid holdout samples, query class/sensitivity, sample size, and mechanism/query or overfit budgets). Thresholdout uses calibrated noise and tracks overfit queries; SparseValidate bounds positive validation responses and transcript description length. | Formal result for statistical data analysis, not a direct RSI/LLM experiment. A hard cap bounds the number of responses, but by itself neither supplies these assumptions nor guarantees useful generalization; finite aggregate alphabets could enter a separate quantitative description-length analysis. | The existing query budget and separate final split are sensible containment boundaries, but aggregate feedback is still information. A proposed leakage probe is meaningful only with an explicit adaptive proposer and feedback interface: on synthetic/authorized fixtures, test whether repeated pass/total changes distinguish a fixture property or label. This diagnoses possible inference under that setup; it is not evidence of leakage or statistical invalidity in the protocol. Protect the final split from proposal adaptation. |

## Synthesis for the current design

The architecture’s strongest reliability move is the authority boundary, not a premise that the verifier is correct: Tier 1 blocks mechanically checkable failures; Tier 2 supplies bounded findings and references; the protected gate validates subject/evidence binding and alone decides release. This makes a false semantic PASS a remaining risk, but prevents it from directly changing criteria or control flow. The cited evaluator studies support keeping the latter distinction explicit.

The design’s output-led context, producer/assessor separation, read-only evidence broker, mandatory challenge cases, and no-verifier-chain rule are directionally consistent with the primary evidence. Separation is contextual rather than statistical independence; new 2026 evidence reports correlated judge errors even across providers in its tested preference tasks. Review-case evidence should therefore include known-good, known-bad, ambiguous, injection, omission, unsupported-claim, near-tie, and counterfactual/order perturbations, with results retained per case and by failure mode rather than summarized only as a pass rate.

For RSI, the literature supports cautious bounded iteration: feedback should point to observable failures, but scorer improvement is not candidate improvement. The evaluator/rubric/referee must remain outside mutation scope; score queries need an explicit budget; raw hidden labels remain private; and final evaluation should be run by the custodian after candidate selection is frozen. Report paired candidate-v-parent outcomes and regression counts, not only aggregate score. Any adaptation to aggregate feedback should be specified before access and evaluated on fresh improvement tasks before the improver itself is changed.

## Candidate M2 evaluation experiments (research proposals, not new requirements)

1. **Judge perturbation matrix:** for each Tier 2 profile, use held-out labeled cases with answer/output order swaps where meaningful, repeated calls, evidence ordering changes, paraphrased obligations, near-ties/ambiguity, and injected instructions in producer content. Report subject-level disagreement, false-advance rate, false-block rate, and abstentions. Freeze test cases/metrics before candidate measurements; keep a separate development set for prompt/rubric iteration.
2. **Authority fault injection:** force semantic assessor outputs to PASS while Tier 1 fails, evidence refs are wrong, subject hashes are stale, response is malformed/uncertain, timeout occurs, or post-review mutation is attempted. Verify all cases remain non-advancing and no cached PASS can release a successor. (This tests the code boundary, not model quality.)
3. **RSI budget/feedback curve:** under the adopted query budget, compare candidate-v-parent paired results on hidden-loop tasks, query-count-limited aggregates, then one custodian-only final evaluation. Track target score, contract regressions, security violations, and query exhaustion across each attempt; stop on predefined thresholds. Keep hidden-final results out of subsequent proposal context. Specifically test whether the approved per-session aggregates allow the proposer to infer individual fixture properties over repeated rounds; this is a leakage probe, not a claim that the protocol is statistically invalid.
4. **Evaluator calibration and correlation:** estimate obligation-level precision/recall/coverage and uncertainty/escalation behavior on challenge cases. Compare repeated calls and distinct model families only as diagnostics; do not treat majority vote as guaranteed independent or as replacement for evidence.

## Scope guard

These are cross-domain analogies, not direct validation of JiuwenSwarm. The empirical LLM-judge papers mostly study open-ended preference/ranking, not infrastructure conformance or scientific interpretation. The reward-model papers study optimization against learned scores, not this exact offline capsule loop. Contamination work mostly concerns pretraining benchmarks, not protected fixtures. Each experiment must be designed for the actual profile and user-approved obligations.
