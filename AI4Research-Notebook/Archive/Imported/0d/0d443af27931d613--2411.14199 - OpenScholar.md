# 2411.14199 · OpenScholar: Synthesizing Scientific Literature with Retrieval-Augmented LMs

Asai, He, Shao, Shi, Singh, … Koh, Hajishirzi · UW, Allen Institute for AI, UIUC, CMU, Meta, UNC, Stanford · arXiv 21 Nov 2024 · 53 pp (18 body)
Read: p1-11 (system, benchmark, main results)

## Summary
An **open** retrieval-augmented LM for literature synthesis: a 45M-paper datastore,
trained retriever and reranker, an 8B model, and an inference loop of **self-feedback**
with extra retrieval and a final citation check. Also introduces **ScholarQABench**
(2,967 expert queries, 208 expert long-form answers, 4 fields).

## How it works (p4-6)
- **Datastore (OSDS)**: peS2o open-access papers, 45M papers, 234M passages of 250 words
  with title prepended
- **Retrieval** from 3 sources: trained bi-encoder (Contriever continually pre-trained on
  peS2o, top-100), Semantic Scholar API via LLM-generated keywords, web search restricted
  to arXiv/PubMed; then a **cross-encoder reranker** fine-tuned on Llama-3-70B relevance
  labels; ≤3 passages per paper; citation counts mixed into the score
- **Inference**: draft y₀ with citation markers → up to 3 natural-language feedback items
  → iterative refinement, retrieving again when feedback asks for missing content →
  **citation verification**: the LM checks every "citation-worthy statement" is supported
  by a retrieved passage and inserts citations post hoc if not
- The same pipeline generates synthetic training data for OpenScholar-8B

## Results
- ⭐ "GPT-4 fabricated citations in **78-90%** of cases" when asked to cite up-to-date
  literature in CS and biomedicine (p1)
- OpenScholar-8B beats GPT-4o by 5% and PaperQA2 by 7% in correctness; citation accuracy
  "on par with human experts"; OpenScholar-GPT4o +12% correctness over GPT-4o (p1)
- Expert pairwise (16 scientists, 108 expert answers): OpenScholar-GPT4o preferred 70%,
  OpenScholar-8B 51%, GPT-4o 31-32% (p1, p3)

## Limitations
- Citation verification is itself an LLM step (post hoc insertion); nothing mechanical
  checks that a quoted passage exists
- Open-access corpus only; evaluation mostly single-turn QA

## Relevance
- REPORT.md §3.5: the 78-90% figure (⚠ GPT-4 **without retrieval**) is the baseline
  failure rate that citation grounding exists to fight
- Contrast with AI4Research: OpenScholar grounds by retrieval and self-check (Tier IV,
  LLM-verified). AI4Research's literature verdict requires the claim text to occur in its
  cited section (case-insensitive substring, `delivery.py:133-139`), and it passes on only
  spans whose slice and SHA-256 re-verify (`:115-132`); both are code checks, but they
  only ever handle verbatim sentences, and no gate fails a claim whose span does not
  re-verify. Retrieval-grounded systems like OpenScholar share the basic defence of citing
  only retrieved text
