# 2506.07446 · Fact in Fragments: Deconstructing Complex Claims via LLM-based Atomic Fact Extraction and Verification (AFEV)

Zheng, Li, Liu, Huang, Jia, Ye, Zhang · BUPT, Jinan University, Fujian Medical University · arXiv 9 Jun 2025 · 32 pp (25 body)
Read: p1-7 (introduction, related work, problem definition); method and results at abstract level

## Summary
A fact-verification framework for **complex, multi-hop claims**. It decomposes a claim
into atomic facts **iteratively** (each round conditioned on facts already verified),
retrieves and reranks evidence per atomic fact, and verifies each with dynamically
chosen demonstrations, then aggregates to an overall verdict. Reports state-of-the-art
accuracy on 5 fact-verification datasets.

## How it works (p3-7)
- Motivation: static decomposition (rule-based or one-shot LLM) accumulates errors;
  surface-level semantic retrieval pulls in noisy evidence; static prompts cannot adapt
  reasoning to claim type (p4)
- 3 modules: **Dynamic Atomic Fact Extraction** (iterative LLM decomposition), **Refined
  Evidence Retrieval** (pretrained reranker filters noise; retrieves similar labelled
  claims as demonstrations), **Adaptive Atomic Fact Verification** (label + rationale per
  fact, fed back into the next decomposition round)
- Formally y_i, r_i = Reasoner(F_i, E_i, A_i) per atomic fact; aggregated to y* (p7)

## Results
SoTA accuracy and interpretability on 5 benchmark datasets (abstract). Specific numbers
not read this session; do not cite them.

## Relevance
- Evidence that the field treats **claim decomposition quality** as the bottleneck of
  verification, and has moved from static rules to iterative LLM decomposition
- Marks the far end from AI4Research's static, rule-based sentence selection: AFEV
  argues rule-based decomposition is exactly what causes error accumulation (p4)
