# 2608.05810 · When Self-Evolution Backfires: Pre-Commit Gating against Skill Contamination in LLM Agents

Shang, Xu, Sun, Xia, Hu, Xu, Zheng · Tencent · arXiv v2 17 Sep 2026 · 9 pp (7 body)
Read: p1-9 in full (body p1-7, references p8-9). There is no appendix in the PDF: the
"Supplementary Material" cited for implementation details and the full git contamination
chain (p5, p7) is not included. Tables extract cleanly; Figure 1 is a plot with no numbers

## Summary
Self-evolving agents distil natural-language skills from their own trajectories and
admit all of them into the context of every later round. On Terminal-Bench 2 this is
**not monotone**: ungated pass@1 rises to a peak at round 3 (62%, 105 skills), then falls
to **50% at R5 with 179 skills**, only 2 pp above its own R1 (p5, Table 1 p6). The paper
calls the peak the **capability-contamination tipping point / phase transition** and
blames it on defective skills that both mislead the agent and serve as reference context
when later skills are distilled. It argues this is **structurally irreversible**: deleting
a bad source skill leaves its descendants. Its fix, **Verifier-as-Gatekeeper (VaG)**,
keeps every new skill invisible (Cold) until it passes three critics and a joint
marginal-gain selection. VaG improves every round to **72% with 37 skills** (p5).

## How it works (p3-5)
- **Formal model** (p3): pool grows as M_r = M_{r-1} ∪ G(S_r), with S_r ~ π(· | M_{r-1});
  G is the admission operator; ungated = G is identity. Injection gain
  g(A, τ) = R(M ∪ A, τ) − R(M, τ)
- **Taxonomy** (p3): *individual* (singleton gain < 0), *combinatorial* (two skills each
  ≥ 0, jointly < 0), *systemic* (the non-monotone curve P(k), peaking at k* = argmax P(k))
- **Irreversibility** (Eq. 5, p3): R(M \ {s}) < R(M \ ({s} ∪ desc(s))), where desc(s)
  are skills whose distillation context contained s. ⚠ This is stated as holding
  "whenever desc(s) contains at least one skill that inherited s's flawed reasoning": a
  conditional, not a proof, and it does not by itself say how large the gap is
- **Trust tiers** Cold ≺ Warm ≺ Hot; only Hot skills enter the system prompt (p3-4, Fig. 2)
- **Gate 1, Cold → Warm, individual harmlessness**, conjunction of 3 critics (p4):
  - *SchemaCritic*, structural validity: deterministic frontmatter check (name, trigger,
    body), ν ∈ {0,1}
  - *ExecCritic*, behavioural harmlessness: single-skill A-B replay on the Holdout split,
    pass if R(M ∪ {s}) ≥ R(M) (ties admitted because the estimate is noisy)
  - *AgentCritic*, semantic consistency: **one LLM call** checking for fabricated facts,
    contradiction with existing skills, or unsafe operations
- **Gate 2, Warm → Hot, combinatorial safety** (p4-5): greedy marginal-gain selection;
  add the Warm candidate with the largest estimated joint gain, keep it only if it
  strictly improves held-out performance, stop when none helps. Each f(H) is the mean of
  3 held-out replays; |W| ≤ 15 per round. The authors say it is a **heuristic with no
  optimality guarantee** (utility neither known submodular nor monotone) (p5)
- **Setup** (p5): TB2 split by difficulty into Event 50 (distillation and evolution),
  Holdout 14 (gate replays only), Test 25 (final evaluation). Backbone Hy3; 5 rounds,
  k = 3 rollouts per task; pass/fail from TB2's deterministic checker, no LLM grader.
  Four configurations: Seed (3 hand-written skills), Ungated, Post-hoc Rollback, VaG

## Results
- ⭐ **Tipping point (Table 1, p5-6)**: Ungated 48% (R1, 35 skills) → 60% (R2, 68) →
  **62% (R3, 105)** → 52% (R4, 141) → 50% (R5, 179). Seed 46%. Hard tier 53% at R2 →
  35% at R5
- **VaG (Table 1)**: 52 → 58 → 62 → 68 → **72%**, Hot pool 5 → 37. At R5, +22 pp over
  Ungated R5 and +10 pp over Ungated's best round; Hard tier 59% (p5-6)
- ⚠ **Significance**: 95% Wilson bands are about 30 pp wide (n = 50, k = 3) and overlap
  at intermediate rounds; the authors say per-round tests are underpowered and rest the
  claim on trajectory shape (p5). One evolution run per configuration is reported
- **Tokens** (Table 1): VaG 0.77-0.94 M per trial vs Ungated up to 1.30 M (p6)
- **Ablation at R5 (Table 2, p6)**: −schema −2 pp (pool 37), −holdout replay **−10 pp**
  (pool 45), −semantic −4 pp (40), −marginal-gain gate **−8 pp** (pool 58). ⚠ The claim
  that the critics catch "largely disjoint" skills (p4, p6) is argued from these drops;
  no per-skill overlap count is reported
- ⭐ **Post-hoc rollback (Fig. 4, p5-7)**: removing the **8** harmful source skills from
  the R5 pool (179 → 171) moves 50.0% → 51.7%. Of the **12.3 pp** peak-to-R5 drop, source
  removal recovers **1.7 pp** (≈14%); oracle full-lineage cleanup recovers **6.7 pp in total**
  (56.7%, ≈54%); **5.6 pp** stays lost even under the oracle. The intro rounds this as
  "recovers only **17%**" (p2), which matches Table 1's rounded 2 of 12 pp, not Fig. 4's
  1.7 of 12.3 (≈14%). VaG at 72.0% is 15.3 pp above the oracle (p7)
- Case: an R3 git-conflict skill seeded two R4 skills (merge, rebase); after source-only
  rollback both stayed and kept failing 4 of 7 git-related Test-25 tasks (p7)
- **Transfer (Tables 3-4, p7)**: frozen R5 Hot pool on Test-25 gives +8 to +16 pp over
  Seed on all 5 backbones (Hy3 32 → 44%). On InterCode NL2Bash (200 tasks): Seed 57.5%,
  Ungated 65.5%, VaG 69.0%; there, **Ungated is still net positive** (+8.0 pp)

## Limitations
- Stated: per-round results are not individually significant (p5); small controlled
  setup, not comparable to ACE or AHE on full TB2-89 (p5); the greedy is a heuristic (p5);
  lineage cleanup needs provenance "a real system lacks" (p7)
- ⚠ Not stated: the tipping point and rollback results are both on **Event-50, the
  distillation split**, which the paper itself calls "an optimistic upper bound" (p5).
  Ungated is never reported on Test-25, so the held-out split shows VaG vs Seed only
- ⚠ One backbone (Hy3) evolves the pools; one run; 14 holdout tasks drive every gate
  decision
- ⚠ The gate is **not deterministic** as a whole: one critic is an LLM call and the replay
  critic is stochastic; only SchemaCritic is deterministic, and it matters least (−2 pp)
- ⚠ "No post-hoc operation can restore the pre-degradation state" (p7) generalises from
  the two removal strategies tested; re-distillation or other repairs were not tried
- Supplementary Material is referenced but absent from the PDF

## Relevance
- **Supports**: admitting a capability to the runtime context only after a gate, not
  deleting it afterwards; its rollback numbers (1.7 of 12.3 pp back, oracle 6.7) are
  direct evidence that post-hoc removal in a mutable pool is weak (p7)
- **Supports**: recording provenance (which entries were in context when an entry was
  made); the paper names its absence as why lineage cleanup fails (p2, p7)
- **Supports**: a joint check at admission time, not only per-item checks
  (−8 pp without Gate 2, Table 2)
- **Does not support**: a *deterministic* gate. Its strongest critic is a stochastic
  replay and one critic is an LLM; the deterministic schema check adds only 2 pp
- **Does not address**: append-only storage, versioned packages or immutability; its
  skills are free-text prompt snippets in a mutable pool, and contamination travels
  through distillation context, which a library of fixed packages may not have
