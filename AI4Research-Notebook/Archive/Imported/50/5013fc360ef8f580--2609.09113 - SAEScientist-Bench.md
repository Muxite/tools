# 2609.09113 · SAEScientist-Bench: Can AI Agents Conduct Autonomous SAE Interpretability Research?

arXiv Sep 2026 · 41 pp (12 body, references at p13)
Read: abstract (p1), p6-10 in full (Table 1, setup, results §5.1-5.4). Task
construction §3 and appendices skimmed only. Code: github.com/Trae1ounG/SAEScientist

## Summary
A benchmark for **closed-loop experimental discovery** in a narrow but fully
instrumented domain: given a target concept, an agent must design contrastive probes,
search a 131K-feature Gemma Scope SAE dictionary for Gemma-2-9B-IT, and submit the one
feature that best represents the concept. Scoring is against curated Neuronpedia expert
features on three axes — dictionary-wide activation rank, selectivity on contrastive
texts, and **causal steering** of generation. Ten frontier agent configurations, 20
tasks, three runs each. The report-relevant finding is not the leaderboard but the
documented cases of agents **misreading their own experimental measurements**.

## How it works (p6)
- Agents run in Cursor (or Codex for GPT-5.6 Sol/Luna) with network off, no step cap,
  free choice of reasoning effort and stopping (p6)
- Post-submission validation is fixed: activations on a frozen evaluation set, then
  causal steering with an intervention scale calibrated on held-out prompts; steering
  outputs rated by a **GPT-4o judge** on anonymised (baseline, steered, random-control)
  triples (p6)
- Composite Overall averages the three axes over 20 tasks; reported as mean ± sd over
  three independent investigations

## Results
- Table 1 (p6): **Expert 85.56** overall (Rank 100, Activation 98.92, Steering 57.75).
  Best agents: Kimi K3 **65.82 ± 1.87**, Claude Opus 5 65.41, Claude Sonnet 5 65.04,
  Grok 4.6 62.93; worst GPT-5.6 Luna 50.27 ± 7.40
- Axis gap: agents approach expert on Activation selectivity (top 92.91 vs 98.92) but
  not on causal Steering (**top 31.47 vs 57.75**) (p7)
- Rank correlation of Overall with the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.2:
  ρ = 0.800 (p7)
- ⭐ **Evidence-interpretation failures** (p8, Table 2 and text):
  - Claude Opus 5 on Portuguese, layer 9: authored matched Portuguese/Spanish/English
    probes, then selected a feature that activates **more on the English control (4.50)
    than on the Portuguese target (3.81)**; overall score collapsed to 13.9
  - GLM-5.2 on Clinical symptom: its final feature fires at **70.25 on a patient record
    with no symptoms**; the agent "erroneously downplays this strong non-symptom
    response as negligible in its report"
  - Counter-case: Opus 5 on Cat correctly built morphological negatives ("Copycat
    killer" 44.00, "Catalytic converter" 24.54) and rejected a substring detector
- Search behaviour (p8-9, Fig. 4): GPT-5.6 Sol averages 115.1 authored probe texts but
  tests 20.2 features; Opus 5 tests 80.2 features over 77.6 texts; Sonnet 5 "frequently
  encounters the Expert candidate during initial retrieval but overlooks it without
  direct validation, relying instead on initial ranking heuristics" (p8-9)
- Generalisation (p9-10): features that looked clean under the agent's own probes
  are inactive on held-out positives and fire on irrelevant controls (Sonnet, Grok,
  Luna); Sol reaches AUROC 1.000 like the expert but with target activations of
  3.5-10.0 vs 14.4-19.4, so its dictionary Rank score is 3.97 vs 100
- Steering (p10): features from Opus 5, Sonnet 5, Grok 4.6 and Luna give **zero target
  relevance across all six prompts** despite strong activation separation

## Limitations
- Benchmark, not a system; one domain (SAE feature discovery), one model under study
  (Gemma-2-9B-IT), 20 tasks
- Steering is judged by GPT-4o; the expert baseline is a curated reference, not an
  upper bound on what is discoverable
- Harness is not held constant (Cursor vs Codex), so model and harness are confounded
  for the two OpenAI Codex entries
- Case-level failures are illustrative single runs from Table 2, not rates; the paper
  does not report how often evidence misreading occurs across all 600 investigations

## Relevance
- ⭐ Strongest **trace-level evidence** in this sweep for the report's "weak at checking"
  claim: agents designed the right controls and then drew the wrong conclusion from
  the numbers they themselves produced (p8). That is a failure of the checking step
  *after* a correct experiment, which is precisely the gap a verification tier is
  meant to close
- The dev-vs-held-out divergence (p9: "disparities in generalization and selectivity
  that remain invisible during internal search") is the same phenomenon Arbor's merge
  gate targets, observed here in a benchmark with a fixed external evaluator
- Sonnet 5's heuristic selection vs Opus 5's active testing of the same candidate
  (p8-9) shows that "ran an experiment" and "used the result" are separable behaviours
  a verification tier should score separately
