# 2608.14590 · Toward Safe LLM Agents: A Survey of Specification, Verification, and Enforcement

Dantas et al. · arXiv Aug 2026 · 28 pp (24 body)
Read: p1-5 (problem, scope, contributions, formal background, definitions), key findings located by search (p8, p11)

## Summary
A **PRISMA 2020 systematic review of 38 studies (2022-2026)** on formally validating LLM
agent **plans**. Splits the problem into 3 coupled stages: **specification** (get a formal
safety property φ from human requirements), **verification** (does plan π satisfy φ,
before, during or after execution), **enforcement** (what to do when it does not, and
does that restore task-level safety). "Each stage introduces independent sources of
error; their composition is the central problem" (Fig. 1, p2).

## Key findings (p1)
1. **Specification bottleneck**: translating natural language into formal specifications
   fails semantically. The measured case: LLM-generated **PDDL achieves high syntactic
   validity but only 24-35% semantic correctness** (p4, citing [7]); the abstract
   generalises this to NL-to-formal translation
2. **Runtime monitoring** is the most mature enforcement, cutting unsafe actions 40-65%
   in controlled settings, without complete guarantees
3. **Verifier tax**: blocking 94% of unsafe actions can still leave <5% safe task
   completion, "because agents exploit alternative unsafe paths". ⚠ The authors state
   it "currently rests on a single study and requires independent replication"; GRADE
   certainty **Very Low** (p3, p8)
4. No approach achieves soundness, scalability, semantic correctness and task-level
   safety together

## Useful content
- Formal background: LTL/CTL/STL/PCTL, model checking, PDDL/STRIPS/HTN (p3-5)
- Plan representations: trace-based, **graph-based** (static analysis, DFA products),
  code-based; horizons: short (5-30 actions) tractable, long (50-500+) beyond current
  verifiers (p5)
- **Safe Success Rate (SSR)**: tasks completed correctly *and* satisfying φ; the metric
  that exposes the verifier tax (p5)
- AgentProof: compiles policies to DFAs and checks agent workflow graphs; across 18
  workflows, **27% had structural defects and 55% failed a human-gate policy**;
  verification under a second for 5,000-node graphs (p11)
