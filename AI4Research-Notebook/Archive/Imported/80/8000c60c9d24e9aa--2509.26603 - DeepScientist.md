# 2509.26603 · DeepScientist: Advancing Frontier-Pushing Scientific Findings Progressively

Weng, Zhu, Xie, Sun, Lin, Liu, Zhang · Westlake University (WestlakeNLP) · arXiv Sep 2025 · 19 pp (10 body)
Read: body p1-9 and discussion (p8, p17)

## Summary
A goal-driven discovery system that starts from a **named human state-of-the-art method**
and tries to beat it over month-long runs. Discovery is modelled as **Bayesian
optimisation** over the space of research methods, with an LLM surrogate model to decide
which ideas are worth an expensive real experiment. Claims to surpass human SoTA on 3
frontier AI tasks.

## How it works (p3-5)
- True value f(I) of a method is a costly black box (a full implement-run-analyse cycle,
  ~10¹⁶ FLOPs for a frontier LLM problem)
- **Findings Memory**: thousands of structured records, each promoted through 3 stages
  of increasing fidelity and cost:
  1. **Strategize & Hypothesize**: new hypotheses scored by a **surrogate model, an LLM
     reviewer**, into ⟨utility, quality, exploration⟩ on 0-100 → "Idea Findings"
  2. **Implement & Verify**: **UCB acquisition** picks one idea; a coding agent (Claude-4-
     Opus) implements it on the SoTA repository in a sandbox with full permissions and
     internet → "Implement Finding" with real results
  3. **Analyze & Report**: only if it beats the baseline → ablations, new datasets, paper
     written via MCP tools → "Progress Finding"
- Setup: 3 SoTA starting points (ICML 2025 Spotlight, ACL 2025 Outstanding, ICLR 2024),
  each **manually reproduced** first; 2 servers × 8 H800 GPUs; Gemini-2.5-Pro for logic
- ⚠ "**Three human experts supervise the process to verify outputs and filter out
  hallucinations**" (p5)

## Results
- Beat human SoTA: agent failure attribution 12.07% → 29.31% (+142.8%) handcraft and
  16.67% → 47.46% (+183.7%) algorithm-generated; LLM inference 190.25 → 193.90 tokens/s
  (+1.9%); AI text detection AUROC 0.800 → 0.863 (+7.9%) and 2× faster (Table/Fig. 3, p6)
- Idea funnel: **>5,000 ideas → ~1,100 implemented → 21 genuine innovations** (p2);
  per-task implemented/progress: 600/7, 196/12, 312/2 (Fig. 4a)
- ~20,000 GPU-hours total (p1); near-linear scaling of discoveries with compute (p2)
- Paper quality: 60% "accept" by the DeepReviewer-14B LLM vs 0% for other AI-scientist
  papers (Table 2); human committee of 3 rated DeepScientist papers 5.00 vs ICLR 2025
  average 5.08 (Table 3, p7)
- ⭐ **~60% of a human-reviewed sample of failed trials terminated by implementation
  errors**; the rest mostly gave no
  improvement or regressed (p8); ~50% of initial implementation attempts hit Claude Code
  internal timeouts (p17)

## Limitations (stated)
- Success rate for innovation "remains exceptionally low, making effective validation
  and filtering the new bottleneck" (p2)
- Implementation errors waste most of the budget; calls for standard sandboxes and
  automated testing (p17)
- Publicly available papers from other systems "may be curated" (Table 2 note)

## Relevance
- REPORT.md §3.6: the clearest measurement of where code-generating research systems
  lose effort (implementation errors), and the authors' own conclusion that validation,
  not ideation, is the bottleneck
- The surrogate model is an LLM judge (Tier VIII) used only to *prioritise*; admission to
  "progress" requires beating a baseline on a real benchmark (Tier II). A good example of
  keeping the weak signal away from the gate
- Human hallucination filtering is undisclosed-in-count hidden labour (survey Table 9)
