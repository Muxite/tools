# 2603.05912 · DeepFact: Co-Evolving Benchmarks and Agents for Deep Research Factuality

arXiv v1 March 2026 · 31 pp (9 body + appendices)
Read: body p2-p9 (protocol, study, results). Artifacts: DeepFact-Bench (944 claims),
DeepFact-Eval.

## Summary
Claim-level factuality of **deep research reports** (DRRs), plus an attack on the idea
that a static expert-labelled benchmark can exist for this task. Its Audit-then-Score
(AtS) protocol makes the benchmark label revisable: a challenger verifier that disagrees
must submit evidence, an auditor adjudicates, accepted revisions update the label, and only
then are models scored.

## How it works
- Task: assign each **verbatim sentence** in a DRR a label in {SUPPORTED, INCONCLUSIVE,
  CONTRADICTORY}, with the full report as context (p3). ⭐ They verify at **sentence
  level deliberately**, "to minimize noise from imperfect sub-claim extraction and ensure
  compatibility with future evaluators" (p3)
- **Micro-gold protocol** (p4): hidden known-answer claims, 25% of every annotation batch,
  1:4 supported-to-unsupported. Unsupported micro-golds are made by injecting errors from a
  three-stage taxonomy — collection (hallucinated reference, misattributed citation),
  analysis (causal inversion, fact merging), generalization (over-generalization, dropped
  qualifiers)
- AtS loop: B_{t+1} = B_t ⊕ ΔB_t, with versioned snapshots, changelogs and periodic
  micro-gold calibration (p5)
- DeepFact-Eval: context extraction from the whole report → breadth-oriented query planning
  → search + summarize → **depth-oriented follow-up questioning per document** → reflect or
  answer (p6). Contrast with VeriScore/SAFE/FactCheck-GPT, which decompose then match
  snippets

## Results
- ⭐ **PhD-level domain experts, working in their own specialties on reports generated from
  their own research questions, score only 60.8% on hidden micro-golds** (p5). Rises
  monotonically to 72.7% → 86.0% → **90.9%** over three audit rounds (p6 Fig 3, p8)
- Human-agent decision flows (p6 Table 1): 22.4% "human learns from correct agent",
  13.3% "human resists being misled", **0.0% "human learns from wrong agent"**,
  2.8% misled by wrong agent
- ⭐ **Excluding adversarial micro-golds, 27.0% of the remaining test claims are naturally
  unsupported** (p8) — the best 2026 base rate for unsupported statements in DRRs
- Verifier leaderboard on DeepFact-Bench (p8 Table 2): traditional pipelines top out at
  58.5% accuracy (FIRE); VeriScore 52.5%, SAFE 55.9%, FactCheck-GPT 55.0%. Repurposed
  deep-research agents: GPT-Researcher 69.1%, SmolAgents 68.8%. **DeepFact-Eval 83.4%**
  (GPT-4.1), 87.2% on GPT-5
- Snippet checkers are high-precision/low-recall (SAFE recall 40.6%); deep-research agents
  the opposite (GPT-Researcher recall 98.9%, precision 66.7%) (p8)
- Scaling GPT-Researcher's search depth *hurts*: 69.1 → 68.3 at $0.18 → $0.28 (p9)
- Transfer: on SciFact 84.6% agreement, ExpertQA only 55.0%, Factcheck-Bench 82.0%; most
  disagreement is classed as annotation divergence, not model error (p9 Fig 5)
- Cost: >400 expert-hours; round 0 is 65.5% of the effort, the three audit rounds 34.5% (p8)

## Limitations
⚠ **Self-reported and non-held-out in the strict sense**: DeepFact-Eval is both the
challenger that shaped the benchmark's labels and the system scored on it. The authors say
they did not run benchmark evolution for the leaderboard because no baseline beat
DeepFact-Eval on micro-gold (p9) — i.e. the incumbent decides when the labels change.
⚠ The authors themselves flag the risk that iterative updates "subtly tune the benchmark to
the reasoning style or retrieval habits of the challenger" and schedule a re-audit only
after 5% of verdicts change (p8).
⚠ The "annotation divergence" category on ExpertQA (42.5% of cases) is adjudicated by the
paper's own experts, so external transfer is measured against a label the paper can move.
⚠ Test split is 621 claims from 15 reports, 143 of them micro-golds (120 adversarial) — the
natural unsupported rate is computed on ~478 claims.

## Relevance
- ⭐ **The most important counterweight to "let a human check it"**: expert one-shot labels
  on DRR claims are 60.8% accurate. That is a direct argument for AI4Research's design
  choice of a mechanical, auditable verdict rather than an appeal to human gold
- ⭐ Sentence-level verification chosen over sub-claim decomposition **because decomposition
  is noisy** (p3) — independent support for AI4Research's regex-sentence unit
- **27.0% natural unsupported rate** is the number the report should use for "rate of
  unsupported statements in deep research reports"
- Its verdict set {SUPPORTED, INCONCLUSIVE, CONTRADICTORY} maps onto AI4Research's 5-way
  table; "inconclusive" is again a first-class outcome, not a failure
- The AtS idea is the strongest available answer to LLM-judge circularity: instead of
  trusting one judge, make disagreements produce an auditable, versioned label change
