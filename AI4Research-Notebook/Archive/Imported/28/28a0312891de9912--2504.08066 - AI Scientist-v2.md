# 2504.08066 · The AI Scientist-v2: Workshop-Level Automated Scientific Discovery via Agentic Tree Search

Yamada, Lange, Lu, Hu, Lu, Foerster, Clune, Ha · Sakana AI, UBC, Vector, Oxford · arXiv 10 Apr 2025 · 69 pp (14 body)

## Summary
The technical report behind the workshop-acceptance result that later appeared in
Nature (see 2606.15497). Relative to v1 it removes the human code template, replaces
linear experimentation with **parallel agentic tree search** run by an **experiment
progress manager**, and adds a vision-language model (VLM) reviewer for figures.

## How it works
- **Ideation** at "grant proposal" level, with Semantic Scholar queried during idea
  formulation to check novelty (p4)
- **Experiment progress manager**, 4 stages with explicit stopping criteria (p5):
  1 preliminary investigation (stop at a working prototype), 2 hyperparameter tuning
  (stop when training curves converge on ≥2 datasets), 3 research agenda and 4 ablations
  (stop when compute budget is spent). After each stage an **LLM evaluator** picks the
  best node to seed the next; replications at stage end give mean and std
- **Node cycle** (p5-7): LLM writes plan + Python → run → error marks node buggy →
  plotting → **VLM critiques the figures**; any issue marks the node buggy. Expansion
  picks buggy nodes with some probability (debug) else best-first non-buggy nodes,
  guided by an LLM. Special node types: hyperparameter, ablation, replication (new
  seeds), aggregation (combine replicates)
- Datasets from Hugging Face `load_dataset`, acknowledged as "somewhat ad-hoc" (p7)
- Writing: single pass, then reflection with a reasoning model; VLM checks
  figure–caption alignment and duplicated figures (p8)

## Results
- 3 manuscripts to ICLR 2025 ICBINB (43 submissions); 1 scored 6, 7, 6 = **6.33**, about
  top 45%; withdrawn by prior agreement (p8-9)
- Authors' internal review: none meets main-conference standard (p9)

## Human involvement actually disclosed (p10)
- Humans picked 3 of ~40 AI-generated ideas, after changing the system prompt once to get
  more applied ideas
- Each idea ran with multiple seeds; humans **selected the best manuscript per idea** "based
  on a careful inspection of its overall coherence and scientific quality", likened to "a
  professor reviewing the work of many students"
- No human edited code, results, figures or text of the chosen manuscripts

## Errors found in the accepted paper by the authors themselves (p11)
- Regulariser description misleading (embeddings vs LSTM hidden states)
- Missing key references (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber 1997)
- Figure 3 caption misread validation loss; **Figure 5 contradicted the paper's own
  claim**
- **~57% overlap between training and test sets** found in the code
- The reported 100% accuracy collapsed when task complexity increased

The workshop reviewers caught none of these (their concerns were justification,
single architecture, experimental breadth, p12). Reviewer #2 gave confidence 2: "quite
likely that the reviewer did not understand central parts of the paper" (p13).

## Limitations (stated, p13)
Workshop not main track; 1 of 3 accepted; workshop acceptance 60-80% vs 20-30% main
tracks; novel high-impact hypotheses and rigorous design choices remain hard.

## Relevance
- ⭐ The strongest concrete example for the report: **a peer-reviewed, accepted AI paper
  whose train/test split leaked 57%**. Peer review (Tier VI) and the LLM/VLM reviewers
  (Tier VIII) did not detect a defect a held-out-data check (Tier II) would. This is
  the case for mechanical gates in 1 sentence
- The tree search's node selection by LLM evaluator vs AI4Research's frozen evaluators
- Result selection across seeds is exactly the "result selection" failure mode in the
  verification-gap survey's Table 9
