# 2310.03714 · DSPy: Compiling Declarative Language Model Calls into Self-Improving Pipelines

Khattab, Singhvi, Maheshwari, Zhang, Santhanam, Vardhamanan, Haq, Sharma, Joshi, Moazam, Miller, Zaharia, Potts · Stanford, Berkeley, CMU, … · arXiv 5 Oct 2023 (ICLR 2024) · 32 pp (11 body)
Read: p1-4 (motivation, programming model, signatures); results from abstract/intro

## Summary
Replaces hand-written prompt templates with a **programming model**: LM calls become
declarative **modules** with natural-language **typed signatures** (e.g. `question ->
answer`), composed in ordinary Python control flow, and a **compiler** ("teleprompters")
optimises the prompts and demonstrations (or fine-tunes small LMs) of the whole pipeline
against a metric, from a few training inputs.

## How it works (p2-4)
- **Signatures**: a tuple of input and output fields with an optional instruction; say
  *what* a transformation does, not *how* to prompt it; they also handle structured
  formatting and parsing
- **Modules**: parameterised versions of techniques like Chain of Thought and ReAct,
  analogous to neural-network layers, used in define-by-run graphs
- **Teleprompters / compiler**: simulate the program on training inputs, bootstrap traces
  of each module, keep those that pass the metric, and use them as few-shot
  demonstrations or fine-tuning data

## Results (p1-2)
- Within minutes of compiling, GPT-3.5 and llama2-13b-chat pipelines beat standard
  few-shot prompting by >25% and >65%, and expert-demonstration pipelines by 5-46% and
  16-40%
- GSM8K quality 33% → 82% (GPT-3.5), 9% → 47% (llama2-13b-chat); HotPotQA 32% → 46%
  and 22% → 41%
- Compiled 770M T5 and llama2-13b competitive with expert-prompted GPT-3.5

## Relevance
- REPORT.md §3.1: the canonical meaning of "compiling" LM programs in the literature is
  **optimisation against a metric**, not checking. AI4Research's "compilers" mean the
  opposite: typed artifacts plus deterministic validation. Worth one sentence so a reader
  who knows DSPy is not misled by the word
- Signatures are the closest mainstream analogue to AI4Research's authoring schemas for
  IntentIR and RequirementIR (typed model-facing contracts), without the independent
  review or provenance re-binding
