# 2504.01848 · PaperBench: Evaluating AI's Ability to Replicate AI Research

Starace, Jaffe, Sherburn, Aung, Shern, Maksin, Dias, Mays, Kinsella, Thompson, Heidecke, Glaese, Patwardhan · OpenAI · v3 7 Apr 2025 · 30 pp (9 body)
Read: body p1-6 (task, reproduction, grading, rules, dataset, rubrics) and results summary

## Summary
Agents must **replicate 20 ICML 2024 Spotlight/Oral papers from scratch**, given the paper
and an author-written addendum but not the authors' code. Grading uses hierarchical
**rubrics co-developed with each paper's authors**: 8,316 gradable leaf criteria in total.

## How it works (p2-6)
- Submission = a repository with `reproduce.sh`. ⭐ **Reproduction runs separately on a
  fresh VM** (Ubuntu 24.04, A10, 12 h cap) so "replication outputs can be distinguished
  from any results hard-coded by the candidate at task-time" (p3)
- Rubric tree: leaves are binary; parents are weighted averages; root = Replication
  Score. 3 leaf types: **Result Match** (graded from reproduction outputs), **Execution**
  (did something run), **Code Development** (does code look correct) (p3-4, Table 1)
- Leaves decomposed until an expert could grade one in <15 minutes; rubrics took multiple
  weeks per paper (p5)
- **Candidate never sees the rubric** (prevents overfitting to criteria) (p2)
- Rules: blacklist of the authors' repos and other replications; a post-hoc monitor found
  10 violations in 646 runs, scored 0 (p4)
- LLM judge (o3-mini-high scaffold) validated on **JudgeEval** against human gold labels:
  **F1 0.83** (p1)
- PaperBench Code-Dev: code nodes only, 85% cheaper grading, weakly correlated with full
  score (r = 0.48)

## Results
- **Best agent Claude 3.5 Sonnet (New): 21.0%** average Replication Score (p1)
- 3-paper subset: ML PhDs (best of 3, 48 h) **41.4%** vs o1 **26.6%**; models do not yet
  beat humans (p2)
- o1 on Code-Dev: 43.4%

## Limitations
20 papers, ML only; LLM judge not perfect (F1 0.83); rubrics are costly to build.

## Relevance
- REPORT.md §3.7 benchmark table
- Two design choices worth borrowing, both close to AI4Research's thesis:
  1. **Hidden grading criteria** fixed before the attempt (AI4Research freezes its
     evaluation policy into the plan hash; PaperBench keeps its rubric from the agent)
  2. **Separate re-execution** in a clean environment so claimed results must be
     regenerated (AI4Research's verification recomputes rather than reads stored verdicts)
