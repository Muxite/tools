# 2605.06365 · From Agent Loops to Deterministic Graphs: Execution Lineage for Reproducible AI-Native Work

arXiv May 2026 · 16 pp (13 body)
Read: p2-13 fully (model, research questions, setup, both result tables, discussion, limitations)

## Summary
Argues the shift from **prompt lineage** (state lives in an evolving transcript) to
**execution lineage** (work is a DAG of artifact-producing nodes with declared dependencies
and hashed execution identities). Its contribution to the report is not the architecture —
which is essentially dbt/Airflow applied to LLM nodes — but the **measurement**: it isolates
*maintained-state quality* from *deliverable quality* and shows a strong loop baseline can
score perfectly on the deliverable while leaving the surrounding artifact state incoherent.

## How it works (p5-8)
- Node v = (I_v, f_v, O_v); edge (u,v) means v may consume **only** artifacts u explicitly
  produced and declared. Admissible context is declared *before* execution rather than
  reassembled from conversation (p5)
- **Execution identity** k_v = h(σ_v, x_v, {k_u : u ∈ pred(v)}) — structural spec, resolved
  input hash, and upstream identities. The paper notes h "need not be cryptographically
  special"; what matters is that identity is a deterministic function of structure, inputs
  and ancestry (p6)
- Identity-based replay: matching k_v restores the persisted artifact; a changed k_v
  invalidates exactly its descendants, leaving unrelated branches untouched (p7)
- Four design principles: explicit dependency declaration, local visibility boundaries,
  identity-based reuse ("proof of equivalence, not similarity of prompts"), and
  **deterministic publication** — one canonical output per node boundary, not whatever was
  last in the transcript (p5, p7-8)
- Determinism is defined as a property of the *substrate*, not the model: reproducible under
  fixed inputs, fixed model+params, fixed tool outputs (p6)

## Results (p9-12)
Domain: a telehealth policy memo. n = 3 repeats per condition, GPT-5.2 at temperature 0.7,
two judge passes per repeat (p10).

**Task 1 — unrelated-branch update** (edit in an adjacent provider-recruiting branch, outside
the memo's dependency path; correct behaviour is to change nothing) (Table 1, p10):

| Condition | Exact preserve | Churn | Contamination | Input tokens | Wall-clock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Loop final update | 0.00 | 0.923 | 0.667 | 11,655 | 33.5 s |
| Loop + edit event | 0.00 | 0.908 | **1.000** | 11,694 | 55.0 s |
| DAG replay | **1.00** | 0.000 | 0.000 | **382** | 7.9 s |

- **30.5× fewer input tokens and 4.2× faster** than the loop baseline; faithfulness 1.0 vs
  0.0, current-state precision 1.0 vs 0.333/0.0 (p11)
- The sharpest result: the loop that **was told what changed** contaminated the memo in 3/3
  runs — worse than the loop that was not. Edit-event awareness without dependency structure
  did not help (p11)

**Task 2 — intermediate criteria edit** (a recommendation-criteria artifact gains a
budget-neutrality constraint; correct behaviour is to propagate downstream, preserve
upstream) (Table 2, p10):
- **All three conditions** reflected the new constraint (1.00) and scored 1.00 faithfulness.
  A final-output-only evaluation would call all three successful (p11)
- Only DAG replay reached 1.00 cross-artifact consistency (loops: **0.50**), 1.00 stable
  artifact preservation, 1.00 downstream propagation recall, 0.00 upstream churn
- Here the DAG was **slower**: 73.6 s vs 38.7/31.1 s (1.9× and 2.36× slower) and produced
  more output tokens (4,311 vs 2,419/1,981), because replay was sequential over two model
  calls (p11-12). The paper refuses the general efficiency claim (p12)

## Limitations
- ⚠ **Not an information-equalised comparison, and the paper says so** (Table 3 p12, p10):
  the DAG condition receives the dependency graph, artifact identities, *and* a
  dependency-derived recomputation plan; the loops receive none of these. It is a
  system-level comparison, so "DAG replay wins" partly restates "we gave it the answer to
  the question being scored"
- ⚠ n = 3 repeats, **two tasks, one domain, one model family**, judged partly by an LLM
  judge. The authors frame it as a controlled mechanism study, not a benchmark (p13)
- Loop baselines are naturalistic, not tuned; the authors concede a custom loop harness with
  explicit state tracking could narrow the gaps (p13)
- The longitudinal claim — that partial inconsistency compounds over many revisions — is the
  paper's actual motivation and is **not measured** (p12)
- The graph must be correctly specified; a wrong dependency edge silently removes the
  advantage (p13)

## Relevance
- REPORT.md §3.3: the best available **quantified** evidence for the freeze's second-order
  benefit. The freeze is usually defended on integrity grounds; this paper shows a separate
  payoff — precise invalidation — and quantifies it (exact preservation 1.00 vs 0.00, 30.5×
  token reduction on the no-op update)
- The distinction between deliverable quality and maintained-state quality is directly
  reusable for AI4Research: repeated revision of a report is exactly Task 2, and the loop
  baselines' 0.50 cross-artifact consistency is what silent rot looks like at n=1
  (cross-reference 2604.00392 Beyond Task Completion, 2601.13217 MR DRE)
- **Load-bearing question**: closer than it first looks, then clearly not. The DAG has a
  *criteria* artifact whose hash feeds every downstream identity (p9, Fig. 1 p8) — criteria
  inside the integrity chain. But those are the *recommendation* criteria being reasoned
  about, not evaluator bindings. And the run's actual grading rubric is deliberately
  **withheld from the runtime**: "does not receive judge-only labels, the correct final
  recommendation, the hidden scoring rubric" (p9). The opposite of sealing evaluator
  bindings into the run
