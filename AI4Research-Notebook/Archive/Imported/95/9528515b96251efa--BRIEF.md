# Reading openJiuwen's documentation properly

You are reading documents, not searching them. **Word search is insufficient and has already cost this project
once**: a `grep` for "admission" and "precondition" over openJiuwen's `symphony` module returned nothing, and
was taken as evidence that no admission gate existed. A 2,734-line subsystem implementing exactly that was
sitting in the same directory under different vocabulary. Do not repeat it.

## How to read

1. **Read whole documents.** Open the file and read it. Do not grep for keywords and report the hits.
2. **Prefer goals over state.** These repositories change weekly, so a fact about today's code decays. A stated
   intention, a roadmap, a design rationale or a non-goal is worth more than a current-state observation. When a
   document distinguishes "current implementation" from "final design", capture **both** and say which is which.
3. **Chinese documents need care.** Many repositories carry `README.md` and `README_zh.md`, or Chinese-only
   design docs.
   - Quote the **original Chinese** for anything load-bearing, then give your own gloss.
   - **Where the English and Chinese versions both exist, compare them.** Divergence is a finding: it usually
     means one was updated and the other was not, and the newer one tells you the direction of travel.
   - Flag every place where the meaning is genuinely ambiguous rather than picking a reading and moving on.
     Say "this could mean X or Y" — that is a useful result, not a failure.
   - Do not trust a machine translation you cannot point at. If you are unsure of a term, say so.
4. **Flag contradictions.** Between a README and the code it describes, between an English and a Chinese
   version, between a status table and what is actually in the tree, between two documents in the same repo.
   These are among the most valuable things you can return.
5. **Distinguish claim from evidence.** "The README says X" is different from "X is true". Mark which you have.

## What we care about

Report anything bearing on these, and say plainly when a repo has nothing on them:

- **Capability reuse** — skills, tools, plugins, capability assets: how they are described, stored, found
- **Contracts** — anything a capability *declares* about itself: inputs, outputs, preconditions, effects,
  permissions, cost
- **Admission and validation** — anything deciding whether a capability is good enough to keep or publish
- **Versioning, lineage, deprecation** — how a capability changes over time, and what happens to the old one
- **Evidence and provenance** — traces, audit trails, claim-to-evidence links, reproducibility
- **Composition** — how capabilities connect, and whether anything composes *systems* rather than functions
- **Research-agent workflows** — literature, hypotheses, experiments, reports
- **Stated non-goals and boundaries** — often the most informative sentences in a document

## Rules

- **Read-only.** Do not modify any repository. Write only your own output file.
- Do not use arXiv. Web access only where your prompt explicitly says so.
- Do not read another agent's output file.
- Do not read `capsule-openjiuwen/notes/` or `DESIGN.md` — they contain conclusions that would bias you.

## Output

Write to the file named in your prompt. Structure:

```
# <your scope>

Repos and documents read, with dates/commits where available.

## What each thing is for
One short section per repo or subsystem: its stated purpose, in its own words, quoted.

## Goals and direction
Stated intentions, roadmaps, "final design" sections, planned-but-absent features.

## Findings relevant to the capsule
Grouped by the bullets in "What we care about" above.

## Contradictions and ambiguities
Including every EN/ZH divergence you found, quoted on both sides.

## What I could not determine
Documents that were missing, empty, stale, or too ambiguous to read confidently.
```

Keep it to what a person would actually use. Quote generously where wording matters; summarise where it does
not.
