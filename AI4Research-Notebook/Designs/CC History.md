# Capability Capsule history

This is a sourced lineage of the CC idea, not a replacement architecture. Dates below are dates printed in source artifacts or recorded in their Git history; folder names alone do not establish chronology. Historical proposals remain useful rationale, but current product authority is the PRD plus the maintained `jiuwenswarm` design package.

## Core thesis in the first presentation material

The September 14 draft report and September 2026 presentation define CC as the project’s answer to the limits of hand-authored capabilities in long-running AI4Research. The goal is a library that the system can build, check, reuse, version, and retire while research sprints continue. The presentation says each research result leads to a design rule, grounding the proposal in prior work rather than presenting a feature list. See the [draft report](../../tundle/ai4research/versions/capsule%20REPORT%20%28before%20overnight%202026-09-15%29.md), especially §§1–2, and the [September presentation](../../tundle/ai4research/versions/AI4Research%20Capability%20Capsule%20Presentation%20-%20Muk%20%28before%20last-quarter%20cut%202026-09-15%29.pptx), slides 1–8.

The early argument has four parts:

- **Task-level success does not certify a reusable capability.** Alita-G motivates self-growing tools, but retaining tools from successful runs can keep tools that did not help, and its tools are not tested after rewriting (slides 6–7). Beyond Task Completion motivates held-out conformance checks for the capability’s own job (slide 9). The short design principle is: “tests check promises, not research answers” (slide 21). This matters because a research step may have no single ground-truth answer even when the tool’s declared behavior is testable.
- **A declaration gives checks a target.** The proposal wraps a capability with typed inputs/outputs, effects, trust, and checks. Admission is supposed to compare what the body promises with what protected observation and tests show (slides 4, 14–15, 21). The declaration is the contract for what can be checked; it is not itself proof that a capability is correct.
- **Reuse requires a managed lifecycle.** Sprints pin exact versions; a new version becomes available to later sprints; versions are append-only; retirement unloads execution but retains the record (slides 8, 23). This protects reproducibility while the library evolves.
- **Planning and building are distinct decisions.** A separate decision agent considers recurring gaps, while a generalist can complete a step without forcing immediate construction. A single sighting does not trigger a build, and “Not building is always a valid answer” (slides 17, 20). Exact typed/effect/trust filters precede similarity ranking (slide 22).

These slides are explicitly a proposal: the deck says “nothing here is built or measured yet” (slide 19). Its implementation-order and evaluation slides identify questions to test, not completed evidence (slides 24–25). The report says DSH is a documented target and “has not been run” (§1). Do not read the presentation as a runtime or measured-gain receipt.

## September 23 presentation, post-presentation record, and Layer 2

The [capability-capsule README](../../tundle/ai4research/capability-capsule/README.md) identifies `Capability Capsule - Presentation.pptx` as exactly what was presented September 23: 26 slides, 39:20 planned. It reports that the talk ran 1 hour 40 minutes including questions and records the presenter’s reaction; those are source-reported session facts. The deck’s thesis was “Flexible in form, strict in verification and quality assurance” (slide 1). It described M1 as deliberately small and falsifiable, with a schema as the common contract, protected control distinct from the library, and code reuse in Symphony/openJiuwen as an implementation hypothesis (slides 3–16, 25–26). Even this deck preserves its own epistemic limit: “That a declared capsule is correct” is listed among claims not made (slide 25).

The file named [Post Presentation](../../tundle/ai4research/capability-capsule/Capability%20Capsule%20-%20Post%20Presentation.pptx) is expressly labeled a “REVISED RECORD, not the deck that was presented” (slide 1). Its companion [Post-Presentation Decisions](../../tundle/obby/Archive/Post-Presentation%20Decisions.md) says it was rewritten after the session from the presenter’s understanding; it records agreement claimed by the author, live feedback, and a list of subsequent decisions, but it is not a separate signed approval receipt. Important clarifications include: capsules point to hashed code rather than containing mutable code; a leaf or composite is one shape at a time; no capsule is its own final judge; and gates/deciders/control code stay outside capsules in that revision. The record’s “nothing that decides” boundary was later explicitly superseded on September 29 (see Directives), so it is historical rationale, not current scope.

The [Layer 2 deck](../../tundle/ai4research/capability-capsule/Capability%20Capsule%20-%20Layer%202.pptx) is dated September 24 and asks what to build first on openJiuwen. It proposes an M1 one-node run, seeded stage capabilities, a separate control layer, a store/gate/evaluator/planner-binder/loader/runner/dispatcher/provider/records package, and hash-pinned bindings (slides 1, 5–16). Slide 19 labels the week-by-week plan “Proposal” and says the schema, policy, and validator were already written “today”; that does not say the runtime was built. Slide 17 explicitly says later capabilities are “Not built, but never blocked.” Its original roadmap was: M1 one node; M2 a few chained nodes; M3 a library of about ten; M4 overlap/merge; M5 spatiotemporal composability (slides 3, 17; also schema v2.10b lines 418–422). This is a proposed milestone ladder, not proof of milestone completion.

The v2.10b schema is dated September 24 and marks itself a workshop draft. Its version preface records successive revisions: v2.8 “start extra small, block only for safety, label for quality”; v2.9 adds “one schema progressively activated, every stage applies capabilities, overlap and merge”; v2.10 adds composition; v2.10a says a capsule points at code; v2.10b adds the promise-to-keep-stable section. The README identifies normative-artifact mismatches still open on September 25 (schema-version label, `identity.code_id` versus `identity.carrier`, and effect-class count). Therefore the version history is valuable design provenance, but its schema and artifacts are not a maintained current contract.

The later schema’s original milestone labels were cumulative ideas, not a delivery-status claim: M1 one node, M2 chained nodes, M3 a small library and quality/standing, M4 overlap/merge/tree, M5 composability. The September 24 Layer 2 deck treated M1 as a one-node end-to-end demonstrator and required preserving future seams, while still labeling the implementation plan a proposal. The October PRD and current design define their own M1 boundaries; they supersede this workshop ladder wherever the milestone names or contents differ.

## Workshop schema: declaration-first, with independent checking

The [Capability Capsule workshop deck](../../tundle/ai4research/capability-capsule/older/Capability%20Capsule%20Workshop%20Deck%20-%20Muk.pptx), titled “Design workshop, 22 September 2026,” and its companion [v1.6 schema](../../tundle/ai4research/capability-capsule/older/Capability%20Capsule%20Schema%20-%20Muk.md) sharpen the idea into a schema. The deck states: “declare first, so every check has a target” (slide 2). It separates declaration, admission, availability, and composition (slide 3); gives a manifest example (slide 4); introduces space/time composability through declared dependencies and undo behavior (slide 5); and assigns each schema fact a consumer (slide 6).

The workshop’s verification design keeps the declaration as the reference and observation as evidence against it. Slide 7 contrasts after-the-fact monitoring with checking before use, while retaining observation for held-out tests and audits. Slides 11–14 describe two trust levels, predicate parity, import, and how existing capabilities could enter provisionally. These are design choices in a workshop draft; they do not establish that every capability should be blocked on every kind of semantic review, nor that the verifier should see task input.

## Post-presentation expansion and supersession

The [v2.10b schema draft](../../tundle/ai4research/capability-capsule/Capability%20Capsule%20Schema%20v2.10b%20-%20Muk.md) is explicitly marked “draft for the workshop.” It expands declaration, provenance, RSI lineage, test/admission, library lifecycle, and composition. The [schema derivation](../../tundle/ai4research/capability-capsule/Capability%20Capsule%20Schema%20Derivation%20-%20Muk.md) records source-to-requirement-to-schema reasoning. These preserve the reasoning path; neither is the maintained product contract.

The dated [obby Directives](../../tundle/obby/Directives.md) show changes as explicit supersessions, not automatic regressions. On September 23 the recorded rule was “nothing that decides is a capsule”; on September 29 the directive says “CC applies across the whole system” and explicitly supersedes that September 23 rule and “no capsule in delivery” (Directives lines 82–89). In that later proposal, any node may be a capsule, while admission, library, librarian, and record stores remain outside because they are not nodes. The same entry makes RSI opt-in, pins dependencies, separates selection of capsules from model routing, and describes CC as a schema connected to tools serving multiple roles. Read the prior rule as superseded, not as a current conflict. These directives are personal design history; the later maintained package and PRD govern implementation.

The September 29 handoff says there was a full-coverage capsule-folder rebuild and explicitly says the next step was team review (HANDOFF line 300). This is provenance of a handoff/review state, not evidence that every contained proposal received product approval. The October 13 scope reset is itself labeled “proposal only; not approved architecture or a delivery claim” (private planning file lines 1–5); use it for rationale, not as a contract.

## Current maintained interpretation

The [jiuwenswarm repository instructions](../../jiuwenswarm/AGENTS.md) and [design-package overview](../../jiuwenswarm/docs/architecture/design-package/README.md) point to one maintained design package and the received PRD. The [CC contract](../../jiuwenswarm/docs/architecture/design-package/2-capability-capsules.md) defines a CC as a reusable module with a declaration and pinned body, distinct from a workflow node or task (lines 11–38). Its central rationale remains: “We cannot check something that claims nothing”; every output is checked against the declaration, checks belong to someone other than the author, and versions are immutable (lines 31–37).

The current verifier boundary is narrower and explicit. D20 authorizes the LLM reviewer to see the pinned declaration and exact output only; input/runtime evidence remains with protected deterministic/host processing (decisions.md lines 89–92). D24 makes criteria answerable from those two items and assigns fidelity to producer obligations, host checks, and benchmarks; it explicitly says “no semantic check establishes fidelity” (lines 100–103). This was a deliberate assurance tradeoff. Current user direction captured in [Implementation Intent](../Sources/Implementation%20Intent.md) prioritizes accuracy and conformance; it does not authorize passing task input into the semantic verifier. Held-out tests challenge correctness, while mandatory deterministic gates stay protected; planner proposals may add compatible gates. A finding or observation is not itself a correctness verdict.

The current design keeps protected decision authority separate: work CCs produce artifacts; verifier CCs assess outputs; deterministic check bundles and a protected gate determine release (2-capability-capsules.md lines 56–66). The planner may propose a graph, but the binder validates and freezes it; mandatory checks remain mandatory (see [planning and freeze](../../jiuwenswarm/docs/architecture/design-package/platform/planning-and-freeze.md) and D29 in [decisions](../../jiuwenswarm/docs/architecture/design-package/method/decisions.md#d29)).

## One-shot experiment as learning evidence

The user’s account, captured in the [October 21 prompt receipt](../Sources/Prompts/2026-10-21-library-amendment-request.txt), says the M1 codebase resulted from sending a design package, PRD, and verification package to Codex 6.1 Sol Ultra for a one-shot build. That is user-reported provenance; the prompt does not give a date or a run receipt, so this review does not infer them. Keep it distinct from the separately documented October 16 one-shot comparison/worker-data replay. The October 14 private plan says that dated trial used generic Leader/SwarmFlow, four fixed roles, and worker-data replay rather than an integrated planner or interactive conversation, and could not establish a Spec Kit winner (CC-PLAN-08, CC-PLAN-13). It is not evidence that this was the user-reported single-shot code build.

Although the related planning package uses “approved” in its folder/title, the inspected preamble limits its authority to coordination and says PRD/design govern product requirements. No authorizing approval receipt was located in this bounded review. Record its stated scope, but mark the approval chain unverified.

The current maintained package makes accuracy the priority (design-package README line 57). It requires bad outputs to stop before downstream use (README lines 55–63); performance or speed cannot compensate for correctness failures. The remaining effectiveness question is empirical: whether CC admission, independent held-out tests, deterministic checks, and protected gates improve correctness on representative tasks without unacceptable false rejection. No historical slide, large unit-test count, or passing artifact alone answers that question.
