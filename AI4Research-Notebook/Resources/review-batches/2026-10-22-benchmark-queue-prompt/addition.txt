## Long queues and reviewable acceptance evidence

Support long, finite, data-driven queues of independently defined cases, executed sequentially as designed. Cases specify visible tasks, supplied resources/setup, ordered actions, timeouts and evidence requirements without hardcoding particular scenarios into the apparatus. Persist progress and correlate case/version, repeat, request, app run and target/build identities. Reconcile interrupted submissions and resume observation without silently rerunning product work. Bound campaign duration/storage, save completed records incrementally, and explicitly account for unfinished, blocked or inconclusive cases. Never skip cases to obtain a passing matrix.

For each case, export a structured record and readable review sheet with **Inputs and Setup** and **Actual Output and Observations**. Retain actual inputs/resource hashes, effective configuration/environment, per-step requests and observations, output artifacts and provenance references, check/gate decisions, downstream action or refusal, terminal reason, timings and limitations. Link authorized criteria to the exact evidence supporting or contradicting them; missing evidence stays explicit. A reviewer must be able to assess the case from these records rather than a generated success narrative or final report alone. Complete any missing designed export through its owning contract; do not settle for a summary or bypass access controls. Report unavailable evidence honestly.

Keep execution, independent benchmark scoring and report generation separate. Ordinary client-validation expectations stay in case files; protected benchmark labels, defect annotations, rubrics and expected answers remain referee-only. Do not expose hidden test specifics through product/model inputs or this generic coder handoff. Support varied workflows, resource conditions and output obligations without embedding the supplied example or its thresholds. Queue recovery is transport/observation recovery, not automatic repair or the later long-horizon benchmark family.
