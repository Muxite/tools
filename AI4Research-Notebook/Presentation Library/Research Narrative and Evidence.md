# Research argument and evidence

## Build around one argument

The Tundle deck-builder workflow asks the presentation and any companion report to carry the same argument, claims, and evidence. A presentation should simplify the report’s path without changing what it establishes. Start with the problem the audience needs to understand; show why the idea follows; then distinguish what the evidence supports from what is still proposed.

Keep one main claim on each slide. A sequence of evidence, interpretation, and design consequence is usually clearer than a slide that mixes literature background, implementation detail, measured result, and recommendation. Add technical detail only where the audience needs it to evaluate the point.

## Use a claim/evidence register

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Claim | The exact statement the slide makes |
| Evidence | Source, location, dataset, or experiment record |
| Status | Measured, source-reported, inferred, proposed, or unresolved |
| Method | Conditions and comparison that produced the result |
| Limitation | What the evidence does not establish |
| Slide | Where the claim appears and how it is cited |

Check every figure and statistic against its primary source or exact run record. Cite it on the slide and keep detailed setup and qualification in the notes or presenter pack. Identify proposals as proposals. Avoid claims that the system verifies, outperforms, or guarantees something when only a design, source review, or partial test exists.

## Use speaker notes to support the talk

Write notes as what the presenter will say, not as a defensive explanation of the slide. Include the time, key spoken explanation, and likely questions where useful. Put essential qualifications in the visible deck when the audience needs them to interpret the claim; speaker notes cannot rescue a misleading slide seen on its own.

The Tundle deck-builder spec requires a takeaway title, source, body, and speaker-note time and script for content slides. The September project deck plan uses a short source footer and puts detail in notes. These are useful house defaults for decks using that workflow, not universal presentation rules.

## Preserve what changed after presenting

Keep the spec, evidence ledger, notes, visual sources, and dated copy of the presented deck together. Record questions, timing, and revisions after the talk. Never rewrite a previously presented artifact in place; carry revisions into the next source version and mark which version was shown.
